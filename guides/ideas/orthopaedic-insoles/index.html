Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Agra, Kanpur, Chennai-Ambur and Kolkata are major footwear clusters that make small runs for new brands.
  • Comfort, orthopaedic and sneaker categories are growing fast as buyers spend more on their feet.
  • BIS quality rules now apply to much footwear, pushing out the cheapest low-quality imports.
  • Sneaker culture has turned shoes into a fashion and collecting category for young buyers.

Headwinds and traps

  • Footwear needs many sizes per design, so stock ties up a lot of cash.
  • Returns for fit often run at 20–30% online.
  • Bata, Campus, Puma and marketplace labels are strong at every price point.
  • Mould and last costs make every new design expensive to develop.

What changed in 2026

Footwear quality rules have pushed out the cheapest imports, and buyers are paying more for comfort. In 2026, the footwear brands that grow own a clear need, such as orthopaedic support, work shoes for standing jobs or sneakers with a design identity, and solve fit with better size guides and exchanges.

Diabetics, older people and those on their feet all day need supportive footwear. Custom insoles and stylish orthopaedic shoes serve them. Doctors and physiotherapists are influential.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with 2–3 designs in your core sizes, sell pre-orders or small batches, and reorder only what sells. Add half sizes and wider fits once you know demand.

Licences you'll need

  • GST registration
  • Udyam registration
  • BIS certification under the footwear quality control orders, where it applies to your product
  • Trademark application
  • Correct labelling of material, size and maker details

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Sizes lock up cash

    Every design needs 6–10 sizes. A slow size can leave you with hundreds of pairs you can't sell at full price.

  • Returns

    Fit problems drive returns, and each one costs two-way shipping. Invest in size charts, fit notes and easy exchanges.

  • Moulds are expensive

    Custom soles need moulds that cost lakhs. Use existing sole designs from your manufacturer until you have proven demand.

  • Big brands own the price

    Campus, Bata and marketplace labels sell good-enough shoes cheaply. You need a clear reason to exist beyond price.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is 300 pairs sold with a return rate below 20%.

  1. Days 1–15

    Choose your niche

    • Pick one buyer and one need, such as office comfort, orthopaedic support or sneakers.
    • Visit a footwear cluster and meet 3–5 manufacturers.
    • Order samples in 2–3 designs.

  2. Days 16–45

    First run

    • Test samples on 20 people for a week each.
    • Produce a small run in your core sizes.
    • Register GST, Udyam and your trademark.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell

    • Launch on your own site and one marketplace.
    • Send pairs to 20 creators in your niche.
    • Offer free exchanges for size.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Check returns by size and design.
    • Reorder the best sellers.
    • Decide on the next design or channel.
