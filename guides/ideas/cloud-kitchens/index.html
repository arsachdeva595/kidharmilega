The 30-second verdict

Only if you can run a tight kitchen and build repeat customers outside the apps. Cloud kitchens have low entry costs, which is why so many open and close within two years. After aggregator commissions, discounts and food costs, a well-run kitchen keeps ₹10–40 on a ₹300 order, so success depends on repeat orders and direct ordering.

Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Ordering food online is a daily habit in metros and fast-growing in tier-2 cities.
  • ONDC buyer apps and direct WhatsApp ordering let repeat customers skip part of the aggregator commission.
  • Office lunch subscriptions and healthy meal plans bring predictable weekday volume.
  • A delivery-only kitchen needs no dine-in space, so rent is far lower than a restaurant.

Headwinds and traps

  • Aggregator commissions and fees commonly total 25–30% of the order, and visibility increasingly depends on paid ads.
  • Ratings swing with a few bad reviews, and a drop in rating cuts orders quickly.
  • Kitchens in the best delivery zones have high rents.
  • Many cloud kitchens shut within two years because they launch too many brands before one works.

What changed in 2026

Aggregators now push paid visibility hard, so a new kitchen that relies only on organic listing struggles. The kitchens doing well in 2026 use the apps to find customers and then move regulars to direct ordering on WhatsApp or ONDC apps, where they keep more of each order.

Multi-brand cloud kitchens, which run several menus from one kitchen, were the big trend a few years ago. Many of those operators have since cut back to one or two strong brands. The kitchens doing well in 2026 pick one cuisine they can own in a small delivery radius, keep the menu short, and use WhatsApp, ONDC apps or office lunch plans to cut their dependence on aggregators.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Launch one tight menu of 8–10 items that travel well, serve a 3–4 km radius, and move repeat customers to direct WhatsApp ordering within the first month.

Licences and government support

Buy vegetables and staples from the wholesale mandi two or three times a week and track food cost daily; a 5-point swing in food cost is the difference between profit and loss.

Licences you'll need

  • FSSAI registration or state licence, depending on turnover
  • Trade licence and fire NOC from your municipality
  • Shops and Establishments registration
  • GST registration (aggregators require it)
  • Udyam registration

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • Stand-Up India: Bank loans from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for new businesses started by women or SC/ST founders.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Discounts are your real marketing cost

    Platform discounts and paid visibility are how most new kitchens get orders, and they often cost more than the profit on the order. If customers only order during a discount, you don't have a business yet.

  • Payouts come with deductions

    Aggregator payouts arrive weekly, but after commissions, ads, penalties and refunds for complaints. Reconcile every payout, because small deductions add up.

  • Wastage

    Over-prepping for demand that doesn't come can waste 5–10% of food cost. Start with a small menu so fewer ingredients can go bad.

  • The second-kitchen trap

    A second brand or kitchen doubles fixed costs long before it doubles orders. Expand only when the first kitchen is profitable without discounts.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹1 lakh in monthly sales with a known repeat rate before you add brands or kitchens.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate the menu

    • Pick a cuisine that is under-served within 3–4 km, based on what aggregator apps show.
    • Cook the menu for 30 people from your target area and ask what they would reorder.
    • Cost every dish to keep food cost at 30–35%.

  2. Days 16–45

    Licences and soft launch

    • Get FSSAI, trade licence and GST, and list on one aggregator.
    • Run a soft launch with friends and nearby offices for honest feedback.
    • Test packaging on real deliveries.

  3. Days 46–75

    Build repeat customers

    • Add a card in every order inviting customers to order on WhatsApp.
    • Pitch weekday lunch plans to 10 nearby offices or PGs.
    • Answer every review within a day.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Measure profit per order after all fees and discounts.
    • Cut the dishes that sell slowly or travel badly.
    • Decide whether to add a second aggregator or ONDC before any new brand.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a cloud kitchen in India?

A single-brand cloud kitchen in a small rented space costs ₹3–8 lakh, including equipment, licences, packaging and launch discounts. Rent is the biggest variable.

How much commission do Swiggy and Zomato charge?

Commission plus fees commonly adds up to 25–30% of the order value, and paid visibility and discounts come on top. Check the current terms with each platform before you set menu prices.

What licences does a cloud kitchen need?

FSSAI registration or licence, a trade licence, fire NOC, Shops and Establishments registration and GST. Aggregators ask for these during onboarding.

Why do most cloud kitchens fail?

The most common reasons are launching too many brands too early, relying on discounts for orders, and food costs creeping above 35% of menu prices.
