Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Young buyers want everyday jewellery they can wear to work, between cheap fashion pieces and expensive gold.
  • Lab-grown diamonds have fallen sharply in price, opening a new category of affordable diamond jewellery.
  • Jaipur, Surat, Mumbai and Kolkata give you access to skilled karigars, stone cutters and manufacturers.
  • Gifting, weddings and festivals keep demand steady through the year.

Headwinds and traps

  • Tanishq, CaratLane, Giva and other well-funded brands spend heavily on marketing.
  • Gold and silver prices swing, which changes your costs and your customers' budgets.
  • Fashion jewellery is copied quickly, and cheap imports flood marketplaces.
  • Buyers need to trust purity and quality before paying for fine jewellery online.

What changed in 2026

Demi-fine silver and gold-plated jewellery and lab-grown diamonds have created a middle market that barely existed five years ago. In 2026, the jewellery brands that grow pick one style and one buyer, prove quality with hallmarks or certificates, and use Instagram to show the pieces being worn every day.

Earrings, necklaces and bangles made from handloom scraps and embroidery offer a unique look. Material costs are low and there's little risk of metal price swings. Each piece is handmade, so train a team early.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with 30–50 designs in one style, produce in small batches, and let Instagram and early buyers tell you which pieces to reorder. Add fine jewellery only once you have a loyal customer base.

Licences you'll need

  • GST registration
  • BIS hallmarking for gold jewellery (with HUID), and registration as a jeweller if you sell gold
  • Certificates from recognised labs for diamonds and lab-grown diamonds
  • Udyam registration
  • Trademark application

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • PM Vishwakarma: For registered traditional artisans (potters, toy makers, tailors and other listed trades): skill training with a stipend, a ₹15,000 toolkit incentive and collateral-free loans at 5% interest.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Ads get expensive

    Jewellery is one of the most competitive categories on Instagram and Google. Without repeat buyers or a strong community, ad costs will eat your margin.

  • Metal prices move

    A rise in silver or gold prices hits your costs immediately. Update prices regularly and don't hold too much metal stock.

  • Plating wears off

    Cheap plating fades in weeks and brings angry reviews. Pay for thicker plating and give care instructions.

  • Trust for fine jewellery

    Selling gold or diamonds online needs certificates, easy returns and buyback promises. Big brands offer all of these.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹2 lakh in sales with a clear best-selling style.

  1. Days 1–15

    Define your style

    • Pick one buyer and one style, such as office wear, minimal gold or bridal fashion.
    • Meet 3 karigars or manufacturers and compare samples.
    • Price your range against 3 competing brands.

  2. Days 16–45

    First collection

    • Produce 30–50 designs in small quantities.
    • Shoot model photos and reels.
    • Register your brand and open your store.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell

    • Send pieces to 20–30 creators in your style.
    • Run small ad tests on your best 5 designs.
    • Offer gift wrapping and personalised notes.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Check ad cost per order and repeat purchases.
    • Reorder the best-sellers.
    • Plan a festive or wedding collection.

What licences and registrations do I need?

GST registration; BIS hallmarking for gold jewellery (with HUID), and registration as a jeweller if you sell gold; Certificates from recognised labs for diamonds and lab-grown diamonds; Udyam registration.

What profit margin can I expect?

60–75% gross margin on fashion and demi-fine jewellery; 10–25% on gold and diamond pieces, where the metal is most of the price. After all costs, expect to keep ₹500–900 per piece; more from repeat and gifting orders.
