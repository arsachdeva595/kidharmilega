Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Buyers are cutting sugar and looking for low-calorie, functional and natural drinks.
  • Quick-commerce apps have become a strong launch channel for new drinks in metros.
  • Co-packers now offer smaller runs for cans, PET bottles and tetra packs.
  • Indian flavours such as shikanji, kokum, jaljeera and regional teas have a loyal audience that global brands underserve.

Headwinds and traps

  • Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Reliance's Campa compete hard on price and own most shop fridges.
  • Minimum order quantities for cans and bottles are high, so your first run ties up a lot of cash.
  • Freight is a large share of the cost because drinks are heavy and low value per kilo.
  • Sales depend heavily on summer; winter months can be very slow.

What changed in 2026

The big soft-drink companies have restarted a price war, so a cheaper cola or lemon drink will not survive. New beverage brands that work in 2026 either own a functional claim (no sugar, electrolytes, gut health), an Indian flavour with a story, or a premium occasion such as mixers and cafés.

Darjeeling, Assam and Nilgiri teas carry strong origin appeal in India and abroad. Flavoured blends, gift boxes and cold brews reach younger buyers. Tea is light and keeps well, which suits online selling. Buy direct from gardens to keep quality and margins.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start in one city with one hero flavour, sell in cafés, gyms and quick commerce first, and prove repeat orders before you pay for wide retail distribution.

Work with a co-packer that already makes a similar drink; they'll handle licensing, filling and quality checks. Buying cans or bottles jointly with other small brands can lower the minimum order.

Licences you'll need

  • FSSAI state or central licence (manufacturing through a co-packer still needs your own licence as brand owner)
  • BIS certification for packaged drinking water or mineral water, if relevant
  • Legal Metrology labelling for packaged goods
  • GST registration
  • Excise licence from the state for any alcoholic drink

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PMFME: 35% credit-linked capital subsidy, up to ₹10 lakh per unit, for micro food-processing businesses. ODOP products get priority.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.
  • Startup India (DPIIT recognition): Recognised startups get easier compliance, tax-holiday eligibility and access to government seed and fund-of-funds programmes.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Minimum orders lock up cash

    A single run of cans can cost several lakh. If the flavour doesn't sell, you are left with stock that expires in 6–12 months.

  • Fridge space is controlled

    The big brands give shops free fridges in exchange for keeping them stocked with their drinks. You'll mostly be on warm shelves unless you pay for placement.

  • Freight is brutal

    A carton of drinks is heavy and cheap. Shipping it across the country can cost more than your margin, so build demand city by city.

  • Seasonality

    Cold drinks sell most from March to June. Plan cash for slow winters, or add hot or all-season products.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is 3,000 units sold, with at least 30 outlets or online buyers reordering on their own.

  1. Days 1–15

    Recipe and costing

    • Develop 2–3 recipes with a food technologist.
    • Run blind taste tests with 50 people from your target audience.
    • Get quotes from 3 co-packers for minimum runs and cost per unit.

  2. Days 16–45

    First run

    • Get your FSSAI licence and test the label claims in a lab.
    • Produce the smallest run you can for one hero flavour.
    • List on one or two quick-commerce apps in your city.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sample hard

    • Give away samples at gyms, offices, colleges and events.
    • Pitch 20 cafés and restaurants and offer sale-or-return for the first month.
    • Collect feedback and reorder data every week.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Calculate margin per unit after all freight and listing fees.
    • Keep the channels where buyers reorder.
    • Decide whether to add a flavour or a second city.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I source tea for my own brand?

Tea gardens and auction centres in Kolkata, Guwahati, Siliguri and Coonoor sell to small brands. Buying direct from a garden lets you tell an origin story, which is what premium buyers pay for.

How much does it cost to start a Flavoured Tea Brand?

₹4–10 lakh for a small start; ₹50 lakh–3 crore for your own line or large co-packer runs, cold chain and distribution. Estimated for a brand using a co-packer, 2–3 SKUs and launching in one metro, 2026 prices.

What licences and registrations do I need?

FSSAI state or central licence (manufacturing through a co-packer still needs your own licence as brand owner); BIS certification for packaged drinking water or mineral water, if relevant; Legal Metrology labelling for packaged goods; GST registration.

What profit margin can I expect?

50–60% gross margin selling online in multi-packs; 25–35% through distributors and retail. After all costs, expect to keep ₹6–12 per unit before marketing.

How long does it take to break even?

Usually 24–36 months for a small setup, if you control costs and build repeat customers early.
