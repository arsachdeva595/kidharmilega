The 30-second verdict

Yes for a B2B-first founder, no if you plan to win retail freezers on day one. Frozen momos were the very first pitch on Shark Tank India, and BluePine Foods won ₹75 lakh because it had already built B2B supply to hotels and restaurants. That is still the route that works: steady orders from cafés and caterers first, retail and quick commerce once you can supply reliably.

Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Quick-commerce dark stores stock frozen snacks in most metros and some tier-2 cities, so a new brand can get freezer space faster than in kirana stores.
  • Cafés, hostels, caterers and cloud kitchens buy ready momos, parathas and snacks to save on skilled kitchen staff.
  • Working households are replacing some home cooking with ready-to-cook food.
  • PMFME gives a capital subsidy to micro food-processing units, which reduces the cost of freezers and machines.

Headwinds and traps

  • Cold-chain logistics costs two to three times more than regular delivery, and one broken link spoils a batch.
  • Retail freezer space is limited and often goes to the big brands that pay for placement.
  • Large players and private labels compete on price in the most popular products.
  • A commercial food-processing setup needs an FSSAI licence, fire safety and a proper cold room, which is a big jump from a home kitchen.

What changed in 2026

Quick commerce changed the entry route. A small frozen-food brand can now get shelf space in dark stores within weeks instead of fighting for kirana freezers. The catch: they expect steady supply, consistent quality and 25–35% margin, so the brands that last usually build volume with cafés and caterers first.

Momos are among the most-ordered snacks in north and east India, and cafés, hostels and caterers buy frozen momos to avoid hiring skilled hands for folding. Quick-commerce apps have also made frozen snacks an impulse buy in metros. The product is simple to make, which is exactly why competition is heavy: your edge has to come from consistency, a filling that stands out, and cold-chain reliability.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start by supplying 10–20 cafés, hostels and caterers who order every week and pay within 15 days. That steady volume justifies a blast freezer and FSSAI licence before you chase retail shelves.

Licences and government support

Buy flour, vegetables and meat from wholesale mandis. The bigger saving is location: set up close to your buyers, because cold transport costs more than most ingredients.

Licences you'll need

  • FSSAI registration (turnover under ₹12 lakh) or state licence for a manufacturing unit
  • GST registration
  • Udyam registration
  • Trade licence from your municipality and fire safety clearance for a commercial unit
  • Legal Metrology declarations on retail packs

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PMFME: 35% credit-linked capital subsidy, up to ₹10 lakh per unit, for micro food-processing businesses. ODOP products get priority.
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • The cold-chain leak

    A single power cut, a late van or a shop that switches off its freezer at night can thaw a whole delivery. Refrozen products lose texture and get returned. Budget for losses and insist on temperature logs from partners.

  • The payment cycle

    Distributors and modern retail often pay in 45–90 days, while you pay for flour and meat in cash. Growing retail sales can drain your bank account faster than slow sales.

  • Freezer space politics

    Retail freezers are small and crowded. Stores prefer brands that pay for placement, fill gaps reliably and don't need chasing. A missed restock can lose you the slot.

  • The scale bottleneck

    Moving from a home or shared kitchen to a licensed unit with a blast freezer and cold room is a jump of ₹20–50 lakh. Plan that step with a PMFME or PMEGP loan well before you run out of capacity.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹1 lakh in sales, mostly from repeat B2B orders, before you invest in a larger unit.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate and source

    • Cook three versions of your hero product and test them with 30 target customers and 5 café owners.
    • Freeze samples for two weeks and check taste and texture after reheating.
    • Get quotes for ingredients, packaging and a deep freezer.

  2. Days 16–45

    Compliance and pilot batch

    • Get FSSAI, Udyam and GST registration.
    • Produce a pilot run of 200–300 packs with proper labels and batch codes.
    • Set up cold delivery for a 5 km radius.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell directly

    • Offer free trial packs to 20 cafés, hostels and caterers; convert them to weekly orders.
    • Sell to households through apartment WhatsApp groups and a weekend stall.
    • Track reorders and complaints for every customer.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Calculate the real cost per pack, including wastage and delivery.
    • Check how quickly B2B customers pay.
    • Decide whether to approach quick commerce or add capacity first.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start a frozen momos business?

A small unit supplying cafés and caterers in one city can start at ₹3–6 lakh for equipment, deep freezers, packaging and licences. A licensed unit with a blast freezer and cold room costs ₹40 lakh or more.

Do I need an FSSAI licence for frozen momos?

Yes. Any food business needs FSSAI registration, and a manufacturing unit above ₹12 lakh turnover needs a state licence. Retail packs also need Legal Metrology labelling.

Who buys frozen momos in bulk?

Cafés, college canteens, hostels, caterers, cloud kitchens and small restaurants are the steadiest buyers. They order weekly and value consistent quality over brand names.

What did Shark Tank India say about frozen momos?

BluePine Foods pitched frozen momos in Season 1, Episode 1, asking ₹50 lakh for 5% and closing at ₹75 lakh for 16% from three sharks. Their strength was an existing B2B and HoReCa business with a clear revenue track record.
