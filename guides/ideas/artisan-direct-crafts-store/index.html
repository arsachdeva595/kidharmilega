Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Home buyers and renters are spending more on decor, and Instagram has made styled homes aspirational.
  • Buyers increasingly want handmade, sustainable pieces over mass-produced imports.
  • India's craft clusters, from Moradabad brass to Jaipur marble and Saharanpur wood, supply global decor retailers.
  • Gifting for weddings, housewarmings and corporate events is a large, steady market.

Headwinds and traps

  • Cheap imported decor sells at a fraction of the price on marketplaces.
  • Fragile pieces break in shipping, and returns are costly.
  • Artisan output is limited and slow, which makes big orders hard.
  • Designs are easy to copy once they sell.

What changed in 2026

Decor buyers now scroll past mass-made pieces and look for a story: who made it, which craft, which town. The brands that grow in 2026 pair one craft cluster with modern design, and sell as much to interior designers and corporate gifting buyers as to individual customers.

Most artisans sell through middlemen who keep a large share of the price. An artisan-direct brand shares more with makers and tells their stories. Corporate gifting, exhibitions and export buyers value this model.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with a small collection from one craft cluster, sell through Instagram and exhibitions, and pitch interior designers and corporate buyers for bulk orders early. Those orders fund your stock.

Licences you'll need

  • GST registration
  • Udyam registration
  • Trademark application
  • Import-export code for export orders
  • GI authorised-user registration if you use a GI craft name

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Vishwakarma: For registered traditional artisans (potters, toy makers, tailors and other listed trades): skill training with a stipend, a ₹15,000 toolkit incentive and collateral-free loans at 5% interest.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Breakage

    Marble, ceramic and glass pieces break in courier networks. Budget for proper packaging and a breakage reserve, or you'll lose money on every broken order.

  • Slow, limited output

    Handwork can't be rushed. A corporate order of 1,000 pieces may need months, so confirm capacity before you accept.

  • Copies

    Once a design sells, cheaper copies appear quickly. Keep releasing new designs and tell the story of your makers.

  • Seasonal demand

    Diwali, weddings and year-end gifting bring a large share of sales. Place production orders months ahead.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹1.5 lakh in sales, including at least one bulk order from a designer or business.

  1. Days 1–15

    Choose your craft

    • Visit one craft cluster and meet 3–5 artisans or cooperatives.
    • Order 10–15 sample pieces in modern designs.
    • Show them to 20 target buyers and 5 interior designers.

  2. Days 16–45

    First collection

    • Register for GST and Udyam.
    • Produce a small collection of the designs people liked most.
    • Shoot styled photos and set up your store.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell

    • Launch on Instagram and at one exhibition or pop-up.
    • Pitch 20 interior designers and corporate gifting buyers.
    • Test your packaging by shipping to friends in other cities.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Check breakage, margins and which designs sold.
    • Plan the festive collection and place orders early.
    • Decide whether to add a second craft or cluster.
