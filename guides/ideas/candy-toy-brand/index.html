Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Festivals such as Diwali, Raksha Bandhan and weddings bring huge demand every year.
  • Corporate gifting and premium boxed sweets are growing fast.
  • Sugar-free, millet and healthier sweets appeal to a new group of buyers.
  • Online and quick-commerce sales let sweet brands reach beyond their neighbourhood.

Headwinds and traps

  • Most traditional sweets have a very short shelf life.
  • Milk, ghee and dry-fruit prices keep rising.
  • Food safety inspections are strict around festivals, and adulteration scandals hurt trust.
  • Established shops have decades of goodwill.

What changed in 2026

Sweet buyers are split: some want traditional mithai from a trusted shop, others want premium, healthier or modern confectionery. In 2026, new sweet businesses do well by picking one side, such as premium gifting, sugar-free, or long-shelf-life packaged sweets, rather than competing with the neighbourhood halwai.

Candy with toys sells in kirana stores and schools. Low prices and fun packaging drive impulse purchases. Safety of toys is essential.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with a small range of your best sweets, sell through festival pre-orders and corporate gifting, and open a shop only when you have steady demand.

Buying milk directly from dairies and dry fruits from wholesale markets such as Delhi's Khari Baoli cuts costs.

Licences you'll need

  • FSSAI licence
  • Trade licence or health licence from the municipality
  • Shops and Establishments registration
  • GST registration
  • Legal Metrology labelling for packaged sweets, including the 'best before' date

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PMFME: 35% credit-linked capital subsidy, up to ₹10 lakh per unit, for micro food-processing businesses. ODOP products get priority.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Short shelf life

    Milk sweets spoil within days. Make to order and plan stock carefully.

  • Festival crunch

    You'll be overwhelmed at Diwali and quiet at other times. Hire temporary staff and plan cash flow.

  • Food safety

    One case of adulteration or illness can ruin your reputation. Test ingredients and keep records.

  • Rising costs

    Milk and ghee prices rise every year. Review prices regularly.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹2 lakh in sales, including one festival or corporate order.

  1. Days 1–15

    Plan your range

    • Choose 8–10 sweets you make best.
    • Taste-test with 50 people.
    • Price against local shops.

  2. Days 16–45

    Set up

    • Get your FSSAI licence and set up a kitchen.
    • Design gift boxes.
    • Line up ingredient suppliers.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell

    • Take festival pre-orders.
    • Pitch corporate gifting to 20 companies.
    • List on one quick-commerce or delivery app.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Calculate margins by product.
    • Cut slow sellers.
    • Plan the next festival season.
