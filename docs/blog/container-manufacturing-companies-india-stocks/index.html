Container Manufacturing Company in India (2026): Who Makes Cargo Containers, Listed Stocks and Supplier Openings

Only a few companies in India actually make ISO cargo containers today: DCM Containers, Kalyani Cast-Tech, BHEL and Braithwaite. Here's the 2026 list, which listed stocks matter, what the ₹10,000 crore CMPS pays, and where small fabricators can fit in.

November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Only a handful of companies in India make real ISO/CSC-certified cargo containers right now. DCM Containers (Faridabad, unlisted) and Kalyani Cast-Tech (BSE SME) are the main ones. BHEL and the railway PSU Braithwaite & Co also make them, and Mazagon Dock and Artson Engineering are reported to be getting started. If you're searching for a container manufacturing company in India for a supply deal, a stock idea or a business opening, this list is short, and the new ₹10,000 crore Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS) is meant to grow it fast.

Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you want three things in one place: who actually makes the boxes, which listed stocks are really connected to the scheme, and where a small fabricator with ₹5–20 lakh can find work in this supply chain.

Key facts

  • 28 October 2026: the Union Cabinet approved the ₹10,000 crore CMPS, running up to FY 2039-40 (PIB release).
  • Per-container support: ₹49,500 per 20-ft box and ₹86,625 per 40-ft box.
  • Capacity target: from about 70,000 TEU to 7.5 lakh TEU a year, roughly tenfold.
  • Early July 2026: India's first Made-in-India EXIM container was unveiled for Maersk at the Maersk-CONCOR ICD in Dadri, UP (PIB). Maersk ordered 1,000 units.
  • China makes 90–97% of the world's containers. India imports about 2 million empty boxes a year, almost all of them Chinese.
  • Guidelines and application portal: not yet notified as of 6 November 2026.

Cargo container manufacturing companies in India: the 2026 list

One warning first. Most "top 10 container manufacturers" directories mix up ISO shipping containers with portable cabins and site offices. They're different businesses. An export box needs Corten (weathering) steel, ISO dimensions, CSC plate certification and impact testing. A site cabin needs none of that. The list below covers cargo container makers only.

1. DCM Containers (Faridabad, Haryana) – unlisted

Run by Rudra Shriram, this company built India's first EXIM container and holds Maersk's 1,000-unit order. Some news outlets credited the "DCM Shriram Group", but according to The Core, DCM Shriram Ltd denied holding the order. So buying DCM Shriram shares won't get you exposure to this order.

2. Kalyani Cast-Tech – listed (BSE SME 544023)

It's the only listed company already producing export containers. It made about 6,000 TEU in FY26. Its 10,000-TEU Shivlakha plant began output in June 2026 and had supplied around 500 boxes by 29 October. It's also building an ICD in Kutch, targeting FY28.

3. BHEL – listed

The heavy-engineering PSU has moved into ISO container fabrication and is one of the bigger names likely to compete for scheme support.

4. Braithwaite & Co – PSU, unlisted

Makes ISO and dwarf containers mainly for the Railways and government buyers.

5. Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders – listed

Reported to have entered container making. Details on volumes are still thin.

6. Artson Engineering (Tata Projects subsidiary) – listed

In talks to build pilot containers using Tata Steel Corten steel. It reported an FY26 loss of ₹10.9 crore, so for now this is an early-stage bet.

Also worth knowing: Tata Steel got a BIS licence for container-grade weathering steel in December 2022, which makes it the obvious domestic raw-material supplier. Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, JSW Group and CONCOR have reportedly shown interest in building or buying Indian boxes.

What the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme pays

The ₹10,000 crore covers about 13 years: 3 years to build plants, then 10 years of production support. Here's the split, as reported from the PIB release and confirmed by several news outlets:

  • Operational support, ₹8,891 crore: ₹49,500 per 20-ft and ₹86,625 per 40-ft container.
  • Interest subvention, ₹859 crore: 3% a year on term loans. Greenfield plants must be at least 1,00,000 TEU a year. Brownfield expansions must add at least 25,000 TEU a year, commissioned within 3 years of in-principle approval.
  • Testing and certification, ₹150 crore: for facilities such as dynamic longitudinal impact testing.
  • Scheme management, ₹100 crore.
  • Land at major ports: leased at 25% of the notified rate.
  • Local content: 70% through FY 2031-32, rising to 90% from FY 2032-33.

Some quick maths: a brownfield plant making 25,000 twenty-foot boxes a year could earn up to about ₹123.75 crore a year in operational support. That's why only big players can use the main incentive. An inter-ministerial steering committee chaired by the Secretary, Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, will approve guidelines and sanction the money. Once the guidelines are notified, check shipmin.gov.in. No official application link exists yet, so ignore anyone selling "CMPS registration".

Can Indian boxes compete with Chinese ones?

Not yet on price alone. Indian-made containers cost 30–40% more, about $700–1,000 extra per box. The ₹49,500 subsidy closes a little over half of that gap. The rest has to come from scale, cheaper inputs and buyers who value a reliable supply. That reliability matters: during the July 2026 West Asia disruption, the cost of an empty box for one Kochi–Iraq shipment reportedly jumped from $1,500 to nearly $50,000.

Where small fabricators can join the supply chain

Seedhi baat: with ₹2.5–20 lakh, you won't be building a 1,00,000 TEU plant. But the scheme explicitly covers ancillary industries, and the 70%→90% local-content rule means container plants will have to source parts in India. That's your opening. The scheme expects about 3,000 direct jobs and 50,000+ indirect ones, and most of the indirect jobs sit in this supplier layer.

Parts a container plant has to buy

  • Corner castings: the cast-steel corner blocks every container needs. This suits foundries in established casting clusters.
  • Wooden or bamboo-composite flooring: plywood-type floor panels made to load specs.
  • Paints and coatings: marine-grade primers and topcoats.
  • Door hardware, locking bars, gaskets, cross-members and sheet pressings: classic light-engineering job work.
  • Testing support, repair and refurbishment: depots near ports and ICDs need welders and inspectors.

If your district already has a metal or engineering ODOP, you're starting with an advantage. Faridabad, home of DCM Containers, has a strong auto components cluster whose pressing and fabrication skills carry over directly. Rajkot's engineering goods foundries are natural candidates for castings, and Delhi's light engineering goods units can bid for hardware and fittings.

Roadmap: how to get onto a supplier list (step-by-step)

  1. Pick one component. Choose one part you can make to drawing, such as door hardware, cross-members or castings, rather than "container parts" in general.
  2. Get Udyam registration and basic quality systems. ISO 9001 and material test certificates are the minimum OEMs expect.
  3. Learn the spec. Get familiar with ISO container standards and CSC requirements, and source Corten steel (Tata Steel holds the BIS licence) rather than ordinary mild steel.
  4. Make samples and approach the makers directly. Contact DCM Containers, Kalyani Cast-Tech, BHEL and Braithwaite procurement teams with samples and a price sheet. Watch for new greenfield plants coming up near major ports.
  5. Use the existing MSME schemes for machinery loans while CMPS guidelines are pending. The interest subvention is aimed at large plants, not small suppliers.
  6. Track shipmin.gov.in for the operational guidelines. The local-content rules will show exactly which parts OEMs must buy domestically.

Paise kaise aayenge: realistic expectations

Orders will start small, because Kalyani's new plant is 10,000 TEU and DCM's Maersk order is 1,000 units. Volume should build from FY28 onwards as new plants get commissioned. Plan for a long approval cycle before you see a first purchase order, and keep your existing auto or engineering customers so you have cash flow in the meantime.

Who this is right for, and who should skip it

Right for you if: you already run or work in a fabrication, foundry or pressing unit, you live within reach of a port, ICD or engineering cluster, and you can wait 12–24 months for OEM approvals.

Skip it if: you have no metalworking background, you need returns within 6 months, or what you actually want is the portable-cabin business. Cabins are a separate, already crowded market with no CMPS support.

Frequently asked questions

Which company makes shipping containers in India?

The main ISO/CSC-certified cargo container makers are DCM Containers (Faridabad, unlisted), which built India's first EXIM box for Maersk, and Kalyani Cast-Tech (BSE SME). BHEL and the railway PSU Braithwaite & Co also make them. Mazagon Dock and Artson Engineering are reported to be entering or piloting container production.

Is DCM Shriram the company making Maersk's containers?

No. The 1,000-unit Maersk order is with DCM Containers, a Faridabad company run by Rudra Shriram, which is unlisted. According to The Core, DCM Shriram Ltd denied holding the order, so its shares don't give you exposure to that container business.

How much subsidy does CMPS give per container?

The Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme, approved by the Cabinet on 28 October 2026, gives operational support of ₹49,500 per 20-ft container and ₹86,625 per 40-ft container. It also offers 3% interest subvention on term loans for qualifying greenfield plants (1,00,000 TEU a year) and brownfield expansions (25,000 TEU a year).

How do I apply for the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme?

As of 6 November 2026 there is no way to apply yet. The operational guidelines and application portal haven't been notified. An inter-ministerial steering committee under the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways will issue them, so check shipmin.gov.in, and don't pay anyone offering 'CMPS registration'.

Which is the best container manufacturing stock in India?

Kalyani Cast-Tech is the only listed company already producing export containers, but it traded at about 48x trailing P/E in late October 2026. BHEL and Mazagon Dock have smaller exposure, and CONCOR and SCI benefit only indirectly. This isn't investment advice, and prices change daily.

Can a small business benefit from India's container push?

Yes, mainly as an ancillary supplier. The scheme covers ancillary industries, and local-content rules start at 70% and rise to 90% from FY 2032-33. That creates demand for corner castings, flooring, paints, door hardware and cross-members, plus repair work at depots near ports and ICDs.
