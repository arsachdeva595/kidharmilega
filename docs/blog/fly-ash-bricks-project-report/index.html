Fly Ash Bricks Project Report 2026: Manufacturing Process, Machine Price, ₹12–25 Lakh Plant Cost, Profit and PMEGP Subsidy

A filled-in fly ash bricks project report for a small Indian unit: the 10-step manufacturing process, machine prices from ₹2 lakh to ₹35 lakh, a ₹25 lakh semi-automatic plant making 8,000 bricks a day at ₹3.80 each, break-even at about 44% capacity, and the PMEGP and 2026 ash-rule details.

This fly ash bricks project report works through a small semi-automatic plant. It costs about ₹25 lakh (land leased, not bought), makes about 8,000 bricks a day at a cost of roughly ₹3.80 per brick, and breaks even at about 44% capacity. The machine on its own costs anywhere from ₹2 lakh for a manual or vibro unit to ₹35 lakh for a fully automatic line, and the manufacturing process takes 10 steps from ash heap to dispatch. Both are covered below. Under PMEGP, a rural general-category founder puts in ₹2.5 lakh and gets a ₹6.25 lakh margin-money subsidy. You apply through the KVIC PMEGP e-portal. If it doesn't load, the scheme details are on myScheme's PMEGP page.

Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you have ₹2–10 lakh of your own, a thermal power plant within reach, and a local construction market that already buys bricks. Every number below is a worked assumption, not a quote. Plug in your own district's prices before you take it to the bank.

  • GST: 12% with input tax credit, or 6% without ITC (details below).

  • New rules in 2026: on 30 January 2026 the Ministry of Power revised its ash utilisation guidelines, replacing the 15 March 2024 version. Each plant now reserves part of its ash for micro and small enterprises at a concessional rate, auctions the rest, and gives away only what's left over (details in the "Is fly ash free?" section below). The manufacturers want clarity on three things: priority access to ash, free versus concessional ash, and whether builders within 300 km must use BIS-compliant products.

What this means for you: ash supply is getting more formal and more competitive. Before you buy a machine, get a written ash allocation from a plant.

Fly ash bricks manufacturing process: step by step

This is how a semi-automatic hydraulic line making about 8,000 bricks a day runs. A manual or vibro unit follows the same steps, but more of the work is done by hand. Timings and moisture levels are indicative, so follow your machine vendor's spec sheet.

  1. Receive and store raw materials. Fly ash comes by tanker or tipper from the plant's silo (dry ash) or ash pond (wetter, lumpier pond ash). Stone dust or sand, cement or lime, and gypsum are stored separately. Keep the ash covered or damp, because dust control is usually a condition of your pollution board consent. At 8,000 bricks a day you'll handle about 12 tonnes of ash and 7–8 tonnes of stone dust daily.
  2. Screen out lumps. Sieve the ash and stone dust to remove stones, clinker and lumps of unburnt carbon. Oversize particles cause cracks and broken corners at the press.
  3. Weigh each batch. Measure every batch by weight or with fixed measuring boxes, using one ratio (our example uses 55% fly ash, 33% stone dust, 10% cement, 2% gypsum). Mixing by eye with shovels is the commonest reason for uneven strength.
  4. Dry-mix, then wet-mix. Load the pan mixer and mix dry for a short time so the cement spreads evenly. Then add water slowly. A common field test: squeeze a handful and it should hold together as a ball without water dripping out. Too wet and bricks stick to the mould. Too dry and they crumble.
  5. Convey to the press. A belt conveyor carries the mix to the press hopper. On fully automatic lines batching, mixing and feeding are linked and controlled from a panel.
  6. Press the bricks. The hydraulic press (or the vibration table on a vibro unit) compacts the mix in the mould and pushes the green bricks out onto pallets. The mould decides the size. Our example uses 230 × 110 × 75 mm, and you can order extra moulds for other sizes and solid blocks.
  7. Air-dry in shade. Leave the green bricks on pallets under the shed, usually for 24–48 hours, until they're firm enough to handle without breaking. Keep them out of direct sun and wind or they'll develop surface cracks.
  8. Water-cure for 14–21 days. Stack the bricks on the curing platform and keep them wet by sprinkling water two or three times a day. Cement-based mixes gain most of their strength in this period. Lime–gypsum mixes gain strength more slowly, so ask your vendor how long they need to cure. Curing is why you need the water tank and the big yard, and why your first sale comes about three weeks after your first pressing.
  9. Test for quality. Send samples from your trial batches to an NABL lab for compressive strength, water absorption and dimension tests against IS 12894. After that, check every lot in-house by counting breakages, checking sizes and doing a drop test.
  10. Stack and dispatch. Stack the cured bricks in counted lots for loading, then dispatch them against orders. Carry about 1–2 weeks of cured stock so you never miss a contractor's slab date.

From day one of pressing to the first payment usually takes 4–6 weeks: about 3 weeks of curing plus your buyers' credit period. That's why working capital sits in the project cost below.

What a machine quote should include

  • Main press with its capacity in bricks per hour, written in the quote, not just said verbally.
  • Pan mixer and belt conveyor. Some low headline prices leave these out.
  • Moulds: how many brick sizes are included, and what an extra mould costs.
  • Pallets: how many come with the machine. You need enough for 1–2 days of output while the bricks air-dry.
  • Control panel, motors and connected load (HP), so you can apply for the right electricity connection.
  • Freight, installation, trial run and operator training at your site.
  • Warranty, a spares list with prices, and the nearest service engineer. A press that sits idle for a week in peak season costs more than the price difference between vendors.
  • GST on the machine, shown separately. Under the 12% GST option you can claim this back as input tax credit. Under the 6% option you can't (see GST below).

Land, power and manpower (semi-auto unit)

  • Land: about 1,000–2,000 sq m (a quarter to half an acre) for storing ash and stone dust, the curing yard and stacking. Leasing beats buying for a first unit.
  • Power: roughly 25–35 HP connected load across the press, pan mixer and conveyor. Confirm with the vendor and apply for an industrial connection early.
  • Water: you need it every day for 14–21 days of curing. Budget for a borewell and a tank.
  • Manpower: 8–12 workers plus one supervisor (usually you).

PMEGP subsidy calculation for a fly ash brick plant

Under PMEGP, a new manufacturing unit can have a project cost of up to ₹50 lakh. The margin-money subsidy is 15% (urban) or 25% (rural) for general applicants, and 25% or 35% for special categories (SC/ST/OBC/minority/women/ex-servicemen and others). Your own contribution is 10% (general) or 5% (special). Some state pages still show the old ₹25 lakh cap, so check the myScheme PMEGP page. On the ₹25 lakh project above:

The subsidy doesn't come to you as cash. The bank holds it in a 3-year term deposit, adjusts it against your loan after the lock-in, and releases it only once you've finished the EDP training. So you still need working capital of your own. You can later take a second PMEGP loan of up to ₹1 crore for upgrading, with a 15% subsidy, which could pay for a move to a fully automatic line.

Scheme status, 5 November 2026: the approval on record covers FY 2021-22 to 2025-26, but the portal is still taking applications in FY 2026-27 and subsidy is still being paid out, including the ₹1,000 crore released on 28 October 2026 (AIR News). The extension to 2030-31 is still only a proposal. If you're ready, apply now rather than waiting for new norms.

Licences, GST and BIS

  • Udyam registration: free at udyamregistration.gov.in. Do this first, because you need it (or a Consent to Operate) to apply for a plant's MSE ash quota.
  • State Pollution Control Board: Consent to Establish (CTE) before construction and Consent to Operate (CTO) before production. The pollution category for fly ash bricks varies by state, so check your SPCB's list.
  • BIS: IS 12894 covers pressed fly ash–lime bricks. IS 13757:1993 covers burnt clay fly ash bricks, which are fired, so it's a different product. Confirm the current versions on the BIS site. Government and big builder buyers within 300 km of a thermal plant increasingly ask for BIS-compliant bricks.
  • GST: Notification 14/2025-Central Tax (Rate), effective 22 September 2025, puts fly ash bricks and blocks (HSN 6815) at 12% (6% CGST + 6% SGST) with ITC. There is also a 6% option without ITC (Notification 02/2022 as amended by 10/2022), and a Tamil Nadu AAR ruling dated 1 September 2025 also gave 12% (TaxGuru summary). As of 24 October 2026 there had been no change to these rates in 2026 (TaxGarden). You can't use the composition scheme, and the registration threshold is ₹20 lakh turnover. Blogs quoting 5% are out of date. Verify on cbic-gst.gov.in. If you're buying a ₹11 lakh machine and 45 bags of cement a day, work out with your CA whether the ITC under 12% is worth more to you than the lower 6% rate.
  • Labour and factory registration: depends on how many people you employ. Ask your District Industries Centre.

Is fly ash free?

Not automatically any more. Many top pages still repeat the old "free ash for brick makers" line. Under the Ministry of Power's revised guidelines of 30 January 2026 (K&S Partners summary), ash is handed out in this order:

  1. Reserved quota for MSEs and local users: each plant sets aside part of its ash for micro and small enterprises. A plant committee decides how much and at what concessional rate, and allocates it through an Expression of Interest (EOI). To qualify you need Udyam registration or a Consent to Operate.
  2. Auction: the remaining ash is auctioned, with a floor price of ₹1 per tonne.
  3. Free, only what's left: ash still unsold after that can be given free to users within 300 km, but you pay the transport.

Under the Ash Utilisation Notification of 31 December 2021, coal and lignite plants must use 100% of their ash over a 3-year cycle, and they pay ₹1,000 per tonne for ash they fail to use. So most plants want takers. Even so, as roads and cement take more of the ash, you'll be competing for it. In practice your real "ash cost" is the concessional rate plus freight on a 50–100 km haul, which is why our model uses ₹500/tonne delivered. Ask the plant's ash management cell when its next EOI opens.

A demand bonus: the same 2021 notification requires agencies within 300 km of a coal or lignite plant to use ash-based products, as long as they cost no more than the CPWD or state PWD schedule of rates. Find out the SOR rate for bricks in your district and price below it.

Launch roadmap: 10 steps, about 4–6 months

  1. Market check (weeks 1–2): find out the price of red and fly ash bricks within 30 km, list 10 contractors and any government projects nearby, and note the PWD schedule-of-rates price for bricks.
  2. Udyam + ash allocation: register on Udyam (free), then visit the nearest thermal plant's ash management cell, apply for its MSE quota through the EOI, and get the allocation and loading terms in writing.
  3. Land: lease 0.25–0.5 acre with road access, water and a three-phase power line.
  4. SPCB CTE: file early, because the pollution consent usually takes the longest.
  5. DPR: use the tables above, or the free UP government format, the UP MSME 1-Connect fly ash bricks project report PDF (business page).
  6. Apply for PMEGP on the KVIC portal (the online application form is a separate page). Choose KVIC, KVIB or DIC as the implementing agency and pick your bank.
  7. Bank sanction + EDP training: the Entrepreneurship Development Programme (EDP) training is compulsory before the subsidy is released.
  8. Machine order and civil work: get three vendor quotes using the checklist above, and insist on installation, a trial run and operator training.
  9. Trial batches: run the 10-step process, cure for 14–21 days, then get bricks tested at an NABL lab against IS 12894.
  10. GST registration, CTO and sales: start with 3–4 anchor contractors on credit terms you can actually afford.

Who should start this, and who should skip it

Start it if you're within about 100 km of a thermal plant, construction is growing in your town, and you can handle labour and B2B collections yourself.

Skip it if the nearest ash source is 200+ km away, red bricks sell below ₹5.50 locally, or you can't keep ₹4–5 lakh of working capital aside. If building materials interest you but ash logistics don't work out, look at stone-based ODOP options like granite cutting and polishing in Bapatla or the Morbi ceramic tiles cluster. Frequently asked questions

Is there a free fly ash bricks project report PDF?

Yes. The UP government's MSME 1-Connect portal has a free fly ash bricks project report PDF with a six-year projection format, at msme1connect.up.gov.in. KVIC also lists model project profiles for PMEGP. Treat any free PDF as a template and swap in your district's actual machine quotes, cement prices, ash freight and brick selling prices before you submit it to a bank.

What is the price of a fly ash brick making machine?

Marketplace listings put manual or vibro machines at ₹2–8 lakh, semi-automatic hydraulic machines at ₹8–15 lakh and fully automatic plants at ₹15–35 lakh. One IndiaMART listing prices 1,500–2,000 bricks-per-hour automatic plants at ₹23–28.5 lakh. These are listings, not verified quotes. Total project cost, with shed, electricals and working capital, runs from about ₹10–12 lakh to ₹40 lakh+, not counting land.

What is the fly ash bricks manufacturing process?

There are ten steps. Store the raw materials, screen out lumps, weigh each batch by ratio (for example 55% fly ash, 33% stone dust, 10% cement, 2% gypsum), dry-mix and then wet-mix in a pan mixer, convey the mix to the press, press it into moulds, air-dry in shade for 24–48 hours, water-cure for 14–21 days, lab-test against IS 12894, then stack and dispatch.

What is the GST rate on fly ash bricks in 2026?

From 22 September 2025, under Notification 14/2025-Central Tax (Rate), fly ash bricks and blocks under HSN 6815 attract 12% GST (6% CGST plus 6% SGST) with input tax credit. A separate 6% rate without input tax credit is also available. Nothing changed in 2026. The composition scheme isn't allowed, and pages quoting 5% are out of date.

How much PMEGP subsidy can I get for a fly ash brick unit?

PMEGP gives a margin-money subsidy of 15% in urban areas or 25% in rural areas for general applicants, and 25% or 35% for special categories, on projects up to ₹50 lakh. On a ₹25 lakh plant, a rural general applicant gets ₹6.25 lakh and puts in ₹2.5 lakh. The subsidy is held in a 3-year term deposit, not paid as cash.

Do thermal power plants give fly ash free?

Not automatically. Under the Ministry of Power's 30 January 2026 guidelines, plants first reserve ash for small enterprises at a concessional rate through an Expression of Interest, then auction the rest at a floor of ₹1 per tonne. Only leftover ash goes free to users within 300 km, and you pay the transport. You need Udyam registration or a Consent to Operate to apply.
