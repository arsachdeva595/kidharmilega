Fly Ash Bricks Project Report 2026: ₹12–25 Lakh Plant Cost, Machinery, Output per Day, Profit and PMEGP Subsidy

A filled-in fly ash bricks project report for a small Indian unit: ₹25 lakh semi-automatic plant, 8,000 bricks a day, ₹3.80 cost per brick, break-even at about 44% capacity, plus the PMEGP subsidy maths and the 2026 rule changes.

November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

This fly ash bricks project report works through a small semi-automatic plant. It costs about ₹25 lakh (land leased, not bought), makes about 8,000 bricks a day at a cost of roughly ₹3.80 per brick, and breaks even at about 44% capacity. Under PMEGP, a rural general-category founder puts in ₹2.5 lakh and gets a ₹6.25 lakh margin-money subsidy. Your application goes through the KVIC PMEGP e-portal. If it doesn't load, the scheme details are on myScheme's PMEGP page.

Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you have ₹2–10 lakh of your own, a thermal power plant within reach, and a local construction market that already buys bricks. Every number below is a worked assumption, not a quote. Plug in your own district's prices before you take it to the bank.

TL;DR: fly ash bricks project report at a glance

  • Project cost: about ₹12 lakh for a manual/vibro unit, ₹25 lakh for a semi-automatic hydraulic unit, ₹40 lakh+ for fully automatic. Land is not included.
  • Output: 3,000–4,000 bricks/day (manual) and about 8,000/day (semi-auto, one shift). These are our assumptions, so confirm them with the machine vendor.
  • Cost per brick: about ₹3.80 (raw material ₹3.45). The assumed ex-factory price is ₹6.00.
  • Break-even: about 3,500 bricks/day. Cash payback is about 2–2.5 years after production starts.
  • GST: 12% with input tax credit, or 6% without ITC (details below).
  • Action: get your DPR ready, then apply on the KVIC PMEGP portal.

Why searches for a fly ash bricks project report are spiking

On 3 November 2026, Google Trends showed "fly ash bricks project report" as a Breakout query over the past 7 days. No single news story explains it. Several things are happening together:

  • Post-monsoon season: building work restarts in October–November. That's when people prepare PMEGP and Mudra files, and every bank asks for a DPR.
  • Less ash going into bricks: the CEA's 2024-25 ash report says 96.34% of ash was used overall. Ash used for bricks and blocks still fell from 490 lakh tonnes (2023-24) to 405 lakh tonnes (2024-25), roughly a 17% drop. Roads (34%) and cement (28%) are taking a bigger share.
  • New rules in 2026: on 30 January 2026 the Ministry of Power revised its ash guidelines. Thermal plants now declare their ash stock every year, offer unused ash free first, and then notify agencies within 300 km. MoEFCC also published a draft amendment to the Ash Utilisation Notification, 2021 on 23 January 2026. As of 3 November 2026 we couldn't confirm that the final amendment has been notified, so check egazette.gov.in for the latest status.
  • NGT case: on 5 August 2026 the NGT asked MoEFCC and the Association of Fly Ash Products Manufacturers to check whether the 2021 notification has been amended (Down To Earth court digest). The manufacturers want clarity on three things: priority access to ash, free versus concessional ash, and whether builders within 300 km must use BIS-compliant products.

What this means for you: ash supply is getting more formal and more competitive. Before you buy a machine, get a written ash tie-up from a plant.

Land, power and manpower (semi-auto unit)

  • Land: about 1,000–2,000 sq m (a quarter to half an acre) for storing ash and stone dust, the curing yard and stacking. Leasing beats buying for a first unit.
  • Power: roughly 25–35 HP connected load across the press, pan mixer and conveyor. Confirm with the vendor and apply for an industrial connection early.
  • Water: you need it every day for 14–21 days of curing. Budget for a borewell and a tank.
  • Manpower: 8–12 workers plus one supervisor (usually you).

Raw material mix and cost per brick

A common fly ash brick mix is about 50–60% fly ash, sand or stone dust, 8–12% cement or lime, and about 2% gypsum. Check the exact ratio against your machine vendor's spec and your lab test results. Our assumptions are a 2.8 kg standard brick (230 × 110 × 75 mm) and local input prices:

Cement is more than half your raw material bill, so a ₹30 rise in the bag price moves your margin straight away. At 8,000 bricks a day you'll need about 12 tonnes of fly ash and about 45 bags of cement every working day. Plan your logistics for that.

  • Break-even: ₹23.3 lakh of fixed cost ÷ ₹2.20 contribution = about 10.6 lakh bricks a year. That's about 3,530 bricks a day, or 44% capacity.
  • Payback: cash accruals (profit + depreciation) are about ₹11 lakh in year 1 and about ₹18 lakh in year 2, so you get your money back in roughly 2–2.5 years of production.
  • Stress test: if the price drops to ₹5.50, year-1 profit falls to about ₹1.2 lakh and break-even jumps to about 57% capacity. Price discipline matters more than the machine you choose.

IMARC claims 15–25% net margins for its model plant. That is a vendor estimate for a 5 crore-brick-a-year plant, not a small unit, so don't put it in your bank file.

PMEGP subsidy calculation for a fly ash brick plant

Under PMEGP, a new manufacturing unit can have a project cost of up to ₹50 lakh. The margin-money subsidy is 15% (urban) or 25% (rural) for general applicants, and 25% or 35% for special categories (SC/ST/OBC/minority/women/ex-servicemen and others). Your own contribution is 10% (general) or 5% (special). Some state pages still show the old ₹25 lakh cap, so check the myScheme PMEGP page. On the ₹25 lakh project above:

The subsidy doesn't come to you as cash. It's credited against your loan account after a lock-in period, so you still need working capital of your own. You can later take a second PMEGP loan of up to ₹1 crore for upgrading, with a 15% subsidy, which could pay for a move to a fully automatic line.

Licences, GST and BIS

  • Udyam registration: free at udyamregistration.gov.in.
  • State Pollution Control Board: Consent to Establish (CTE) before construction and Consent to Operate (CTO) before production. The pollution category for fly ash bricks varies by state, so check your SPCB's list.
  • BIS: IS 12894 covers fly ash–lime bricks. Government and big builder buyers within 300 km of a thermal plant increasingly ask for BIS-compliant bricks.
  • GST: Notification 14/2025-Central Tax (Rate), effective 22 September 2025, puts fly ash bricks and blocks (HSN 6815) at 12% (6% CGST + 6% SGST) with ITC. There is also a 6% option without ITC, which a Tamil Nadu AAR ruling has confirmed (TaxGuru summary). Blogs quoting 5% are out of date. Verify on cbic-gst.gov.in.
  • Labour and factory registration: depends on how many people you employ. Ask your District Industries Centre.

Is fly ash free?

Often the ash itself is, and the freight isn't. The Ash Utilisation Notification of 31 December 2021 requires coal and lignite plants to use 100% of their ash over a 3-year cycle. Plants pay ₹1,000 per tonne for ash they fail to use. The Ministry of Power's guidelines of 30 January 2026 make plants offer unused ash free before anything else. In practice you'll pay transport, and on a 50–100 km haul that is your real "ash cost". As roads and cement take more of it, you'll be competing for the same ash.

Launch roadmap: 10 steps, about 4–6 months

  1. Market check (weeks 1–2): find out the price of red and fly ash bricks within 30 km, and list 10 contractors and any government projects nearby.
  2. Ash tie-up: visit the nearest thermal plant's ash management cell and get its availability and loading terms in writing.
  3. Land: lease 0.25–0.5 acre with road access, water and a three-phase power line.
  4. Udyam + SPCB CTE: file both together. The pollution consent usually takes the longest.
  5. DPR: use the tables above, or the free UP government format, the UP MSME 1-Connect fly ash bricks project report PDF (business page).
  6. Apply for PMEGP on the KVIC portal. Choose KVIC, KVIB or DIC as the implementing agency and pick your bank.
  7. Bank sanction + EDP training: the Entrepreneurship Development Programme (EDP) training is compulsory before the loan is released.
  8. Machine order and civil work: get three vendor quotes and insist on installation, a trial run and operator training.
  9. Trial batches: cure for 14–21 days, then get bricks tested at an NABL lab against IS 12894.
  10. GST registration, CTO and sales: start with 3–4 anchor contractors on credit terms you can actually afford.

Who should start this, and who should skip it

Start it if you're within about 100 km of a thermal plant, construction is growing in your town, and you can handle labour and B2B collections yourself.

Skip it if the nearest ash source is 200+ km away, red bricks sell below ₹5.50 locally, or you can't keep ₹4–5 lakh of working capital aside. If building materials interest you but ash logistics don't work out, look at stone-based ODOP options like granite cutting and polishing in Bapatla or the Morbi ceramic tiles cluster. Frequently asked questions

Is there a free fly ash bricks project report PDF?

Yes. The UP government's MSME 1-Connect portal has a free fly ash bricks project report PDF with a six-year projection format, at msme1connect.up.gov.in. KVIC also lists model project profiles for PMEGP. Treat any free PDF as a template and swap in your district's actual machine quotes, cement prices, ash freight and brick selling prices before you submit it to a bank.

How much does a fly ash brick plant cost in India?

A manual or vibro unit costs about ₹10–12 lakh in total, with ₹3–8 lakh of that for machinery. A semi-automatic hydraulic plant costs about ₹22–30 lakh, with ₹8–15 lakh of machinery. Fully automatic lines start around ₹40 lakh. These totals leave out land, include shed, electricals and working capital, and are indicative ranges, not vendor quotes.

What is the GST rate on fly ash bricks in 2026?

From 22 September 2025, under Notification 14/2025-Central Tax (Rate), fly ash bricks and blocks under HSN 6815 attract 12% GST (6% CGST plus 6% SGST) with input tax credit. A separate 6% rate without input tax credit is also available for bricks, which a Tamil Nadu AAR ruling has confirmed. Pages quoting 5% are out of date.

How much PMEGP subsidy can I get for a fly ash brick unit?

PMEGP gives a margin-money subsidy of 15% in urban areas or 25% in rural areas for general applicants, and 25% or 35% for special categories, on projects up to ₹50 lakh. On a ₹25 lakh plant, a rural general applicant gets ₹6.25 lakh and puts in ₹2.5 lakh. A rural special-category applicant gets ₹8.75 lakh and puts in ₹1.25 lakh.

Do thermal power plants give fly ash free?

Usually the ash is free or very cheap, but you pay the transport. The 2021 Ash Utilisation Notification fines plants ₹1,000 per tonne of unused ash, and the Ministry of Power's 30 January 2026 guidelines make plants offer unused ash free first. Freight over 50–100 km is your real ash cost, so get a written supply tie-up before buying machinery.

What is the profit per fly ash brick?

In our worked example, raw materials cost about ₹3.45 per brick and the total variable cost is about ₹3.80. At an assumed ex-factory price of ₹6.00, each brick contributes ₹2.20 towards fixed costs. Net profit works out to about 10% of sales in year one and 14% in year two. If local prices fall to ₹5.50, profit almost disappears in year one.
