Warli Art on Pot: Patterns, Which State It's From, Wall Hangings and How to Sell Them

How to paint Warli art on a pot, what the motifs mean, and how to turn pots, wall hangings and rangoli designs into a small product line, with estimated costs, pricing and the GI-tag rules for Maharashtra's Palghar craft.

November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

To do Warli art on a pot, coat a clean terracotta pot in a dark base (terracotta red, brown or black), sketch the layout lightly in pencil, then paint the stick figures, triangles and circles in white acrylic with a fine brush. Finish with a matte varnish. Warli comes from Maharashtra, from the tribal belt of Palghar district (once part of Thane). It got its GI tag in 2014.

Warli art is from which state? Origin, GI tag and age

  • State: Maharashtra. The Warli and Malhar Koli tribes of the North Sahyadri range made it. The core belt is Palghar district: Dahanu, Talasari, Jawhar, Mokhada and Palghar. Incredible India (Ministry of Tourism) also mentions parts of Gujarat, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu.
  • GI tag: Adivasi Yuva Seva Sangh (AYUSH), Dahanu, filed the application on 7 June 2011, and it was registered on 31 March 2014. A PIB document from March 2026 also gives 2014. You can check the entry on the GI Registry public search.
  • Age: Disputed. Some sources say it may date to the 10th century CE. Others claim 2,500–3,000 BCE, and Incredible India links it to the Bhimbetka rock art. Treat any single date as unsettled.
  • Traditional method: Women painted it on walls coated with geru (red earth) and cow dung, for weddings, harvests and festivals. The white was rice-flour paste with gum, applied with a chewed bamboo stick.
  • Modern Warli: In the 1970s Jivya Soma Mashe began painting Warli as art rather than ritual. He received the Padma Shri in 2011 and died on 15 May 2018 near Dahanu.

The market is real. At the Tribes Art Fest in New Delhi, Tribune reported on 16 March 2026 that a Warli painting by Rajesh Chaitya Vangad sold for ₹6.5 lakh. Total festival sales passed ₹1.25 crore.

Warli art patterns: what each shape means

  • Circle: sun and moon
  • Triangle: mountains and trees
  • Square: sacred enclosure or land
  • Human figure: two triangles joined at the tip, with a circle head and stick limbs
  • Tarpa dance: a spiral of dancers around a tarpa (wind instrument) player
  • Chauk (Devchauk or Lagnachauk): the central square. The mother goddess Palghat is drawn inside the Devchauk.

Put a small printed card with these meanings in every box you sell. Buyers pay more when they understand the story, and almost no shop listing explains it.

How to do Warli art on a pot, step by step

Materials (about ₹600–900 for a starter kit): a terracotta or clay pot, sandpaper, a damp cloth, acrylic paints (white plus a base colour), brushes in sizes 000–2, a pencil or white chalk pencil, and matte or satin varnish. Most tutorials, including this guide from cypaint, use acrylic instead of rice paste because rice paste doesn't last on pots.

  1. Clean and dry the pot. Wipe off the dust, lightly sand any rough patches and let it dry fully, ideally overnight.
  2. Base coat. Apply two coats of terracotta, brick red, brown or black. Or keep the raw clay colour, which already looks like geru.
  3. Plan the layout to fit the shape. Tall pots suit a vertical procession of figures. Round pots and matkas suit a circular tarpa dance. Put bands of triangles or dots at the rim and the base.
  4. Sketch lightly. Draw guide lines for the bands first, then place the main scene.
  5. Paint the outlines in white. Use a 0 or 00 brush. Keep the figures thin and stiff, because that is the Warli look.
  6. Add details. Trees, birds, huts, dot borders. Leave negative space instead of filling every gap.
  7. Seal it. Once the paint has cured for 24 hours, apply one or two coats of matte or satin varnish.

Can a Warli pot hold water or plants?

  • Drinking-water matka: Paint only the outside, and never varnish the inside. Keep in mind that painting and sealing the outside blocks the evaporation that keeps a matka cool, so sell it as decor or say clearly that it won't cool as well.
  • Planter: Seal the inside with a waterproof sealant, keep a drainage hole and put a saucer underneath. Otherwise moisture seeping through the clay will lift the paint.
  • Kalash and diyas: These are fine for puja decor. Keep paint away from the wick area of diyas.

Easy version for kids and school projects

Use a small pot, a black or brown base and a white paint marker in place of a brush. Draw just three motifs: a sun circle, a row of triangle mountains, and five triangle-figures holding hands around the pot. It takes about 30–40 minutes.

Warli art wall hanging and rangoli ideas

Wall hangings: Painted terracotta plates, 8–12 inch MDF rounds, small hanging matkas strung on jute rope, or a Devchauk panel on canvas board. MDF rounds are the easiest to ship because they're flat and don't break.

Rangoli: Lay down a dark base (brown or maroon rangoli powder, or geru), then draw the Warli figures in white. A circular tarpa dance around a diya makes a strong Diwali centrepiece. Rangoli isn't a product you can sell, but Warli rangoli stencils are, and so are rangoli workshops at societies.

The GI tag: who can call it "Warli" and who can't

The GI is held by AYUSH, Dahanu. Under the GI Act, only producers from the defined geographical area can register with the GI Registry as authorised users, and that needs the backing of the registered proprietor. If you're from the Palghar belt and work with Warli artists, ask AYUSH and the GI Registry about authorised-user registration. Then you can legitimately sell "GI-tagged Warli painting".

If you're not from there, you have two honest options:

  • Source from Warli artists: Commission Warli artists from Dahanu or Jawhar at fair rates, credit them by name on the card, and sell as their work. This is the strongest story for corporate buyers.
  • Label it honestly: Sell your own painted pots as "Warli-inspired" or "Warli-style". Never use the GI logo or call them authentic GI Warli.

Government background on the craft is on Vikaspedia.

Who buys: corporate gifting and other channels

  • Corporate gifting: HR teams and gifting agencies want Diwali, Women's Day and client gifts that look Indian and handmade. Pitch 50–500 unit orders with a branded sleeve and a story card about the artist. Pitch in September for Diwali. For 2026 the window is nearly over, so target Christmas, New Year and Women's Day orders.
  • Instagram and WhatsApp catalogues: Reels of the painting process do well, as the YouTube "warli art patterns" breakout shows.
  • Exhibitions and melas: Craft bazaars, society Diwali melas, and the Tribes Art Fest model.
  • Cafés, resorts and interior designers: Large matkas and wall panels for decor contracts.
  • Workshops: Pot-painting workshops at ₹500–1,200 per head (our estimate) use your own stock as kits.

Launch roadmap: 6 steps

  1. Weeks 1–2: Make 20 test pots in three sizes and time each one. Find a kumhar who can supply 200+ pots a month with low breakage.
  2. Week 2: Decide whether you're going the GI/artist-partner route or the "Warli-inspired" route, and write your labels to match.
  3. Week 3: Register on Udyam (free), and get GST registration if you'll sell through marketplaces or to corporate buyers.
  4. Weeks 3–4: Shoot photos, print story cards and test your packaging with a drop test before shipping.
  5. Month 2: List 10–15 products on Instagram, a WhatsApp catalogue and one marketplace. Send sample kits to 20 HR or gifting contacts.
  6. Month 3 onwards: Add wall hangings and workshop kits. If you need working capital, look at Mudra (Shishu) loans or PMEGP.

Who should start this, and who should skip it

Right for you if: you have roots in Palghar or Thane, or links to Warli artists. Also if you can paint neatly or manage artists, or you have a corporate network for gifting orders.

Skip it if: you want factory scale, can't handle breakage and returns, or plan to sell machine-printed "Warli" as handmade.

Frequently asked questions

Warli art is from which state?

Warli art is from Maharashtra. It was created by the Warli and Malhar Koli tribes of the North Sahyadri range. Its heartland is Palghar district, in Dahanu, Talasari, Jawhar, Mokhada and Palghar talukas, which were once part of Thane district. Incredible India also mentions parts of Gujarat, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu. Warli painting received its GI tag in 2014.

Which paint is best for Warli art on a pot?

Acrylic paint works best on terracotta and clay pots. The traditional rice-flour paste doesn't last on a curved clay surface. Use a dark base coat such as terracotta, brown or black, paint the motifs in white with size 000–2 brushes, and seal everything with matte or satin varnish once it has cured for 24 hours.

Can I store drinking water in a Warli painted matka?

Only if you paint the outside alone and never varnish or paint the inside. Even then, paint and varnish on the outside reduce evaporation, so the matka won't cool water as well. If you sell painted matkas, call them decor or tell buyers clearly that the cooling effect will be lower.

What are easy Warli art patterns for beginners?

Start with a circle for the sun, a row of triangles for mountains, and figures made of two triangles joined at the tip with a circle head. Arrange the figures holding hands in a ring for a simple tarpa dance. Add dot borders at the rim and base of the pot.

Can I sell my own painted pots as GI-tagged Warli art?

Generally, no. The GI for Warli painting is held by Adivasi Yuva Seva Sangh, Dahanu. Only producers from the defined area who register as authorised users can use the GI. If you're outside that area, sell your pots as Warli-inspired, or commission and credit Warli artists from Palghar.
