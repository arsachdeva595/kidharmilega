Phulkari GI Tag: What It Protects, Who Can Use It and How to Start a Phulkari Business in Punjab

Phulkari's GI tag was issued on 22 October 2010 (certificate no. 145). It is held by PSIEC, covers Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan, and is valid until 13 March 2035. This guide covers what the tag protects, how to become an authorised user, and indicative costs and margins on dupattas and suits.

November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

The Phulkari GI tag was granted under certificate no. 145, dated 22 October 2010 (GI application no. 27, filed 14 March 2005). The registered proprietor is Punjab Small Industries & Export Corporation Ltd. (PSIEC). The tag covers Phulkari embroidery from Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan and is valid until 13 March 2035. If you have roots in Patiala, Amritsar, Tarn Taran or another Phulkari belt and want to build a real business on this craft, yeh page tumhare liye hai. Below you'll find what the GI protects, how to become an authorised user, and what a dupatta or suit actually costs to make and sell.

Google Trends shows searches for "phulkari gi tag" up +1,600% over the 7 days to 2 November 2026. "Balkar Sidhu phulkari" is up +40% on YouTube search over the same window.   • Amritsar events. The Tribune reported a Phulkari workshop for foreign visitors at Attari on 27 October 2026, along with a ramp show and stalls at a Swadeshi Mela.

2010 or 2011? Which date is correct

Some sources disagree. A Lok Sabha answer and several websites give 2011, and one source says 21 October 2010. The registry certificate date is 22 October 2010, so use that one. An older Tribune report said the tag covers Punjab plus Ambala and Hisar in Haryana, but the registry record lists Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan. The Punjab State Council for Science & Technology IPR page also lists Phulkari among the state's GIs.

What the Phulkari GI tag protects, and what it doesn't

The GI is registered for handicraft embroidery. Traditional Phulkari uses a darning stitch worked from the back of handwoven khaddar cotton with untwisted silk floss (pat). A Bagh is the densest version, where the embroidery covers almost the whole cloth. The GI identifies this craft from this region, and in law only the proprietor and registered authorised users should be using the name for GI-registered goods.

What the GI does not do on its own is stop machine-embroidered "Phulkari" from flooding the market. Enforcement depends on someone filing complaints and taking action, and there has been very little of that. Bazaar mein jo "Phulkari dupatta" ₹500 mein mil raha hai, woh almost certainly machine-made hai.

Why the GI hasn't helped artisans yet

  • Earnings fell. Artisans told The Tribune that their income has dropped since the GI tag, mainly because of cheap machine copies.
  • Few people know the GI exists. An earlier ResearchGate study found that none of the artisans it interviewed knew about the GI. A 2026 study reported by ClarityUPSC found very low awareness among both artisans and shopkeepers.
  • Artisans are slow to try new products. In the Tribune piece, a PAU professor estimated that only 10–20% of women artisans are willing to try new products. PSIEC's Senior DGM Rajdeep Kaur pointed to trade-fair support and to making gifts and everyday items.

The 2026 study suggested holograms or digital tracing, honest "hand vs machine" labels, cooperatives that cut out middlemen, and help with authorised-user registration. That list is also a business opportunity. Agar tum yeh gap fill karo, toh tumhara brand stand out karega.

Handmade vs machine-made Phulkari: how buyers tell the difference

  1. Turn it over. Hand darning leaves small, regular dots and short stitches on the back. Machine work usually shows a dense bobbin-thread mesh or a backing layer.
  2. Look for variety. According to the Tribune, machine copies usually come in only 3–4 designs. Handmade pieces have many intricate geometric patterns, and a single piece can take months.
  3. Check the sheen. Floss stitches run long and catch the light in different directions. Machine thread looks flat and uniform.
  4. Price reality check. A densely hand-embroidered dupatta can't sell for a few hundred rupees once labour time is counted.

How to become an authorised user of the Phulkari GI tag

Producers can register as authorised users of an existing GI with the Geographical Indications Registry in Chennai, which is part of Intellectual Property India. The steps below are based on our understanding of the GI Rules and were not confirmed in this month's research. Check the current forms and fees on ipindia.gov.in before you file.

  1. Prove that you're a producer. You need to be an artisan, a producer group or a business that gets Phulkari made in the registered area (Punjab, Haryana or Rajasthan). Collect artisan IDs, a list of workers, photos of work in progress and your address proof.
  2. Contact PSIEC. As the registered proprietor, PSIEC's consent or support for your application helps it go through.
  3. File Form GI-3. This is the authorised-user application. Attach a statement explaining how your goods meet the registered specification (hand embroidery, technique, materials).
  4. Publication and opposition. Accepted applications are published in the GI Journal, and anyone can oppose them within the notice period.
  5. Registration. Once you're registered, you can use the GI name and logo on goods that meet the specification. Track the renewal date, because your registration can't run past the GI's own validity, which currently ends on 13 March 2035.

Tip: if you file as a cluster of 20–50 artisans under one producer company or SHG federation, one set of paperwork covers the whole group, and buyers trust a group label more.

Paise kaise aayenge? D2C sales through Instagram, WhatsApp and your own site keep the most margin. Expect 25–35% net after shipping, returns and ads. Wholesale to boutiques pays 15–25% net but in volume. Wedding orders (trousseau Baghs, sets for the bridal family) are your highest-ticket sales.

Setup budget for a 20-artisan hub-and-spoke unit

  • Working capital for the first 80–120 pieces: ₹2–5 lakh
  • Floss, fabric stock and quality-check tools: ₹40,000–80,000
  • Photography, website/catalogue and packaging with hand-made labels: ₹50,000–1 lakh
  • Two trade-fair or exhibition stalls in year one: ₹60,000–1.5 lakh
  • Total: roughly ₹3.5–8 lakh. Breakeven in about 12–18 months if you sell 60–100 pieces a month by month 9.

Funding routes worth checking: Udyam registration (free), PMEGP margin-money subsidy through KVIC/DIC, Mudra loans for working capital, and artisan cards from the Development Commissioner (Handicrafts), which open up fair stalls and scheme access. Confirm current limits at your District Industries Centre.

Patiala and the ODOP link

Patiala is Phulkari's best-known retail market and is usually listed against Phulkari in Punjab's One District One Product listings. Confirm the current ODOP entry with the Patiala DIC before you count on any ODOP-linked support. Embroidery clusters also run through Amritsar, Tarn Taran (one unit there employs 200–300 women, per the Tribune), Bathinda and Haryana districts. Phulkari business launch roadmap

  1. Months 0–1: Meet 15–25 karigars, test each with one sample piece, and fix piece rates in writing. Register on Udyam.
  2. Months 1–2: Pick 3 hero products, for example a medium dupatta, a suit set and a gift stole. Build a catalogue of 20–30 designs. Don't copy the 3–4 machine designs.
  3. Months 2–3: Start the authorised-user process with PSIEC and the GI Registry. In the meantime, label everything "Hand-embroidered, made in [district]" with the artisan's name.
  4. Months 3–6: Sell through Instagram and WhatsApp, put up 1–2 exhibition stalls, and approach boutiques in Chandigarh, Delhi and Ludhiana.
  5. Months 6–9: Prepare your wedding-season pipeline (October–February). Take Bagh orders with a 50% advance.
  6. Months 9–18: Add QR-code authenticity tags, try diaspora orders in Canada and the UK, and reach breakeven.

Who should start this, and who should skip it

Start if: you have family or community links to women artisans, you can fund 60–90 days of working capital, and you enjoy telling customers the story behind each piece.

Skip if: you want fast, uniform volume (that's the machine market, and margins there are thin), you can't do quality checks in person, or you're not comfortable managing a team that works from home. For more district ideas, browse the Kidhar Milega homepage.

Balkar Sidhu "Phulkari": not the GI tag

If you searched "balkar sidhu phulkari", you're probably looking for the song. Balkar Sidhu is a Punjabi singer, and his track "Phulkari" came out on T-Series in 2005. Frequently asked questions

When did Phulkari get its GI tag?

Phulkari's GI certificate is no. 145, dated 22 October 2010. The application (no. 27) was filed on 14 March 2005. Some sources, including a Lok Sabha answer, give 2011, but the official GI Registry record shows 22 October 2010. The registration is valid until 13 March 2035.

Who is the registered proprietor of the Phulkari GI tag?

Punjab Small Industries & Export Corporation Ltd. (PSIEC) is the registered proprietor. Artisans and businesses who want to use the GI name officially need to register as authorised users with the GI Registry, ideally with PSIEC's support.

Which states does the Phulkari GI tag cover?

According to the GI Registry record, the geographical area is Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan. An older news report mentioned only Punjab plus Ambala and Hisar in Haryana, but the registry lists all three states, so treat that as the correct coverage.

Is machine-made Phulkari covered by the GI tag?

The GI is registered as a handicraft embroidery in Class 26, so machine-embroidered copies don't match what it describes. In practice, enforcement has been weak. Machine copies are widely sold, usually in just 3–4 designs, and artisans say this has cut their earnings since the tag was granted.

How can an artisan become an authorised user of the Phulkari GI?

In general, producers in the registered area file Form GI-3 with the GI Registry. They attach proof that they are producers and a statement showing their work meets the specification, and PSIEC's support helps. The application is published in the GI Journal before registration. Check the current forms and fees on ipindia.gov.in before filing.

Is Balkar Sidhu's Phulkari related to the GI tag?

No. Balkar Sidhu is a Punjabi singer whose song 'Phulkari' was released on T-Series in 2005. He is also the AAP MLA for Rampura Phul, elected in 2022. We found no connection between him and the Phulkari GI tag.
