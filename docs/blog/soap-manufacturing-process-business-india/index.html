Soap Manufacturing Process: Cold, Hot and Factory Methods, Licences, Machinery Cost and Margins in India

Soap is made by mixing oil with lye (saponification), whether you use the cold process, the hot process or a factory line of noodles, plodder and cutter. Here's how each one works, plus the COS-8 licence, 5% GST, ₹ machinery costs, PMEGP funding and realistic margins for a first-time Indian founder.

October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

Every soap manufacturing process rests on one reaction, saponification. Fat or oil reacts with an alkali (sodium hydroxide for bars, potassium hydroxide for liquid soap) and gives you soap plus glycerin. Small makers do this by the cold process, the hot process or melt-and-pour. Factories turn oil into soap noodles, then mix, mill, plod, cut and stamp the bars. If you want to sell what you make, toilet soap counts as a cosmetic in India. That means you need a manufacturing licence (Form COS-8) from your state drug controller, applied for through the system linked on the CDSCO cosmetics page.

YouTube has plenty of videos showing how to stir lye. Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you also want the business side: licences, GST, ₹ setup costs, margins and a launch roadmap.

TL;DR

  • Process: oil + lye → soap + glycerin. Cold process bars need 4–6 weeks of curing. Hot process bars are usable within about a day to a week. Melt-and-pour skips lye completely.
  • Licence: apply on Form COS-5 and receive Form COS-8 from the State Licensing Authority. Guides say it takes about 45 days. It's valid for 5 years and covers up to 10 products in one category.
  • GST: 5% on toilet soap since 22 September 2025. 18% on liquid hand wash, body wash, detergents and laundry bars under 500 g.
  • Money: a handmade micro-unit costs roughly ₹2.5–6 lakh. A semi-automatic toilet soap line costs roughly ₹15–25 lakh in total (our estimates, explained below). PMEGP can subsidise 15–35% of the project.
  • Action: read the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 before you buy a single machine.

The soap manufacturing process: the chemistry in two minutes

Every oil needs a different amount of alkali to turn fully into soap. That figure is its saponification (SAP) value. Coconut oil needs more lye than olive oil, for example. You use a SAP chart or a lye calculator to work out how much lye your recipe needs. Most makers also deliberately use 5–8% less lye than the full amount (called "superfatting") so no harsh free alkali is left in the bar.

  • NaOH (caustic soda) makes hard bars.
  • KOH (caustic potash) makes soft or liquid soap.
  • Glycerin is the by-product. Handmade soap keeps it in the bar. Big factories often recover it and sell it.

Cold process soap, step by step

  1. Weigh everything on a digital scale accurate to 1 g. Guessing is how people get lye burns.
  2. Add NaOH to water, never water to NaOH. Do it in a ventilated area and let the solution cool.
  3. Melt and blend your oils, for example coconut, rice bran, castor and a hard butter.
  4. Pour the cooled lye into the oils and stick-blend until it reaches "trace" (thick like custard).
  5. Add essential oils, clays or herbal powders. Pour into moulds and insulate.
  6. Unmould and cut after about 24 hours. Cure on racks for 4–6 weeks.
  7. Check pH and moisture before packing. Record the batch number and date.

How toilet soap is made in a factory

Large makers use kettle (batch) or continuous saponification. Premium brands often use the fatty acid route: the oil is split and distilled into fatty acids, which are then neutralised with NaOH for tighter quality control. After that, the line looks like this:

  1. Vacuum drying: the neat soap is dried into small pellets called soap noodles. Most small Indian units skip the first two stages and simply buy noodles.
  2. Amalgamator: perfume, colour and additives are mixed into the noodles.
  3. Milling: roll mills make the mass smooth and even.
  4. Plodder: a screw extruder compresses the soap into a long, dense bar.
  5. Cutter and stamping: the bar is cut into billets and pressed into a logo shape.
  6. Wrapping and sealing, followed by quality checks on pH, moisture and fragrance.

Drying and plodding use the most power, so check your electricity load before you sign a shed lease. Liquid soap is simpler: surfactants, water and additives are blended in a mixing vessel.

Lye safety: don't skip this

  • Wear chemical goggles, nitrile gloves and full sleeves. Keep children and pets out of the room.
  • Use stainless steel or PP containers only. Lye eats aluminium.
  • If lye splashes on your skin, rinse with running water for at least 15 minutes. For eye contact, rinse and get to a doctor immediately.
  • Store NaOH sealed and labelled, away from food items.

Licences for soap and cosmetics manufacturing

1. Cosmetic manufacturing licence (COS-5 → COS-8)

Toilet soap is regulated as a cosmetic under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. You apply on Form COS-5 and the State Licensing Authority (your state drug controller, not CDSCO) grants Form COS-8. Commercial guides put the timeline at about 45 days. The licence is valid for 5 years and one application can cover up to 10 products in one category. We found no exemption for home-scale makers, so selling from your kitchen without a licence is a risk.

Applications go through cdscoonline.gov.in, which is linked from the CDSCO cosmetics page. Many states route you via CDSCO's SUGAM registration first, so confirm the entry point with your state drug control office. Fees vary by state. Delhi's forms and fees PDF is a useful example.

No factory yet? Take a loan licence (apply on COS-6, granted as COS-9) and get your product made in someone else's licensed unit. Most first-time founders should start this way.

2. Rules you must follow after you get the licence

  • The Cosmetics (Amendment) Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 513(E), July 2025) let state authorities suspend or cancel licences after a hearing. They also tightened record-keeping and the meaning of expiry and "use before" dates.
  • CDSCO's 18 May 2026 public notice, as reported by secondary sources, says cosmetics cannot make treatment claims. So "neem soap" is fine on your label, but "cures acne" is not.

3. BIS IS 2888

IS 2888:2004 is the toilet soap standard and sets Grades 1, 2 and 3. Grade 1 is high-grade, fully saponified, milled soap. As of 31 October 2026, we could not confirm a mandatory BIS Quality Control Order for toilet soap. Many founders still test against IS 2888 because institutional and tender buyers ask for it.

4. GST and basics

Since 22 September 2025, toilet soap has been taxed at 5%, down from 18% (Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate); see the government explainer). Liquid hand wash, body wash and detergents stay at 18%, according to secondary sources. A West Bengal advance ruling kept laundry soap bars under 500 g at 18%. Beyond GST, you'll need Udyam registration and GST registration. Ask your state pollution control board whether you need a consent to establish.

Paise kaise aayenge: margins and breakeven

Here's an illustrative 100 g handmade herbal bar (our estimate):

  • Oils, lye and actives: ₹22–35
  • Essential oil and herbs: ₹5–12
  • Box, label and wrap: ₹8–15
  • Cost per bar: about ₹35–60

D2C prices for these bars usually run ₹120–200. After 5% GST, shipping, marketplace fees or a 30–40% retailer margin, a realistic net margin is around 20–35%. Machine-made bars from noodles sell for much less per bar, so margins are thinner (often single digits to about 15%) and you make money only through volume and distribution. Expect breakeven in roughly 12–24 months, with most of that time spent building repeat buyers, not producing soap.

PMEGP funding for a soap unit

Soap and cosmetics units qualify as manufacturing under PMEGP, which covers projects up to ₹50 lakh. The subsidy is 15% (urban) or 25% (rural) for general-category applicants. It rises to 25% (urban) or 35% (rural) for special categories such as women, SC/ST, OBC, minorities and ex-servicemen. Your own contribution is 10% (5% for special categories) and a bank loans the rest. You apply on the KVIC PMEGP portal. A semi-automatic line of about ₹20 lakh in a rural area could therefore get ₹5–7 lakh of margin-money subsidy.

Who should start and who should skip

Start if you can wait 6–12 months for repeat customers, you're comfortable with paperwork and batch records, and you have a local ingredient or community to sell to.

Skip (for now) if you want quick returns, you can't afford the licensed premises and qualified technical staff the Cosmetics Rules require, or your plan is to compete on price with ₹30 supermarket bars.

Launch roadmap: first 120 days

  1. Weeks 1–3: Make 10–15 test batches at home. Finalise 3 recipes and record their pH.
  2. Weeks 3–5: Get Udyam and GST registration. Decide between a loan licence and your own unit. If you're borrowing, file for PMEGP.
  3. Weeks 5–12: Apply on COS-5 (or COS-6), get the premises ready, and get samples lab-tested against IS 2888.
  4. Weeks 10–14: Design compliant labels: ingredients, batch number, expiry, manufacturer and licence number, and no treatment claims.
  5. Weeks 14–17: Produce your first licensed batch (allow 4–6 weeks of curing for cold process). Sell through 2 local shops, 1 marketplace and Instagram.
  6. From week 17: Track cost per bar and repeat rate every month. Add a second SKU only once the first one sells again.

Frequently asked questions

What is the soap manufacturing process in simple words?

Soap is made when an oil or fat reacts with an alkali. Sodium hydroxide gives you bars and potassium hydroxide gives you liquid soap. The reaction is called saponification and it produces soap plus glycerin. Small makers use the cold process, the hot process or melt-and-pour. Factories dry the soap into noodles, then mix, mill, extrude it through a plodder, cut, stamp and pack it.

Do I need a licence to make and sell soap from home in India?

Yes. Toilet soap is regulated as a cosmetic under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. You apply on Form COS-5 to your state drug controller and receive Form COS-8. We found no exemption for home-scale makers. If you don't have your own premises, you can take a loan licence (COS-6/COS-9) and get your soap made in a licensed unit.

What is the GST rate on soap in India?

Since 22 September 2025, toilet soap has been taxed at 5% GST, down from 18%, under Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate). Liquid hand wash, body wash and detergents remain at 18%, according to secondary sources. A West Bengal advance ruling also held that laundry soap bars under 500 g attract 18% GST.

How long does cold process soap take to cure?

Cold process soap is usually unmoulded and cut about 24 hours after pouring. It then needs to cure for 4 to 6 weeks so the excess water evaporates and the bar hardens and becomes milder. Hot process soap is cooked at about 80–100 °C and can be used within a day to a week. Melt-and-pour soap is ready the same day.

How much does it cost to start a soap manufacturing business in India?

By our estimates, a loan-licence brand or handmade micro-unit can start with about ₹2.5–6 lakh. A semi-automatic toilet soap line making 200–500 kg a day needs roughly ₹8–18 lakh of machinery, or about ₹15–25 lakh in total with working capital. PMEGP can subsidise 15–35% of an eligible project. Always get written quotes from vendors.

Is BIS certification mandatory for toilet soap?

IS 2888:2004 is the Indian standard for toilet soap and sets Grades 1, 2 and 3. As of 31 October 2026, we could not confirm a mandatory BIS Quality Control Order for toilet soap, so check the BIS website for the latest position. Many makers still test against IS 2888 because institutional buyers and tenders often ask for it.
