How to Start Mushroom Farming at Home in India: ₹50k–3L Setup, Monthly Yield and Profit

Start with oyster mushrooms in one spare room. Setup costs about ₹35,000–65,000, the first harvest comes in 3–4 weeks, and you can book DMR Solan training from November 2026. Here are the room-by-room setup, a cost table, break-even maths and an honest look at morel farming.

October 31, 2026 · 9 min read

The easiest way to start mushroom farming at home in India is with oyster mushroom in one spare room. You grow it in pasteurised wheat or paddy straw packed into polybags. Setup costs about ₹35,000–65,000, and the first harvest comes roughly 3–4 weeks after spawning. Get trained before you spend money. ICAR-Directorate of Mushroom Research (DMR), Solan lists its batches on its official training calendar. Buy spawn only from a supplier on DMR's government spawn supplier list or its private supplier list.

Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you have a spare room, ₹50,000 to ₹3 lakh, and want a product you can sell in your own town within 10 days of harvest.

TL;DR

  • Start with: oyster mushroom. It grows most of the year and forgives beginner mistakes.
  • Setup: about ₹35,000–65,000 for one 10×12 ft room of around 150 bags per cycle (our working estimate).
  • Realistic profit: about ₹5,000–13,000 per cycle, or ₹3,000–8,000 a month from one room once your selling is sorted. This is side income, not a salary.
  • Break-even: about 8–12 months.
  • Upgrade later: seasonal button mushroom in an insulated room, ₹1.2–3 lakh.
  • Skip for now: morel (gucchi). There's no farmer protocol for it in India yet.
  • Action: register for a DMR Solan training batch.

Who should start, and who should skip

Start if: you have a room that stays dark and can be kept humid. You can give it 1–2 hours a day. You already know local sabzi sellers, restaurants or dhabas, or you're willing to go and meet them.

Skip if: you expect to sell everything online, you can't be home for 2–3 waterings a day, or your area has no buyers within an hour's travel. Fresh mushrooms last only about 7–10 days, so whoever you can't sell to locally won't be your customer.

Room-by-room setup

1. Prep area: courtyard, terrace or shed

This is where you chop, soak and pasteurise the straw. You need a 200-litre drum, a gas burner or chulha, buckets, a tarpaulin and drainage. Keep it outside the growing room so dirt and contamination stay out.

2. Spawn-run room: dark and still

Freshly spawned bags sit here for about 15–20 days while white mycelium spreads through the straw. You need darkness, a temperature of roughly 22–28°C, and racks or hanging ropes. Mesh on the windows keeps out flies.

3. Fruiting room: humid, some light, fresh air

This can be the same room as the spawn run if you only have one. Fruiting needs about 80–90% humidity, some indirect light, an exhaust fan for fresh air, a fogger or sprayer, and a thermometer-hygrometer (₹500–1,000) so you aren't guessing.

How to start mushroom farming at home: 10-step oyster roadmap

  1. Get trained. Book a DMR Solan batch (dates below) or a course at your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK). Paise bachao, mistakes avoid karo.
  2. Book spawn 1–2 weeks ahead from a supplier on DMR's government list or private list. Fresh spawn is white and smells like mushroom. Reject any bag with green, black or slimy patches.
  3. Chop the straw (wheat or paddy) into pieces of about 2–5 cm.
  4. Soak it in water overnight, for about 12–16 hours.
  5. Pasteurise it. At home, the hot-water method in a drum is the safest. Some KVKs teach a chemical soak using carbendazim and formalin. Formalin is toxic, so use it only at the exact dose your trainer gives you.
  6. Drain the straw to the right moisture. When you squeeze a fistful, only a few drops should come out.
  7. Spawn in layers. Alternate straw and spawn inside the polybags, tie the bags tight and punch small holes in them.
  8. Run the spawn in the dark for about 15–20 days, until each bag turns fully white.
  9. Fruit the bags. Slit or remove the polythene, spray water 2–3 times a day and give the room light and fresh air.
  10. Harvest when the cap edges start to curl, usually 3–4 weeks after spawning. You'll get 2–3 flushes. Spent straw makes good compost or vermicompost.

Running cost per cycle: straw ₹800–1,600, spawn ₹1,500–3,000, polybags ₹400–600, fuel ₹600–1,000, electricity ₹500–1,000 and packing ₹800–1,500. That adds up to about ₹5,000–8,500.

Paise kaise aayenge: 150 bags at 0.7–1 kg each gives 100–150 kg per cycle. Wholesale prices often sit around ₹80–120/kg and direct retail around ₹150–250/kg (both are estimates, so check your local mandi). At an average of ₹120–140/kg, revenue is about ₹12,000–21,000. After costs and roughly 10% spoilage, you keep ₹5,000–13,000 per cycle. With staggered batches you can run 5–6 cycles a year, so the unit breaks even in about 8–12 months.

Some blogs promise "₹40,000/day" success stories. Treat those as anecdotes from large farms, not as what one room will earn.

Model B: seasonal button mushroom (₹1.2–3 lakh)

Button mushroom needs a room you can hold at 14–18°C. Budget roughly ₹40,000–80,000 for insulation, ₹35,000–50,000 for an AC (often unnecessary in a North Indian winter), ₹30,000–50,000 for 3–4 tier racks, and ₹15,000–30,000 for a humidifier and controls. Don't make compost at home, because the smell and the chicken manure will cause trouble with your neighbours. Buy ready spawn-run compost instead. A common rule of thumb is 15–20 kg of mushroom per 100 kg of compost.

The compost price decides your margin, so get a quote before you build anything. Keep compost, casing soil and electricity below about half of your expected revenue per crop. Plan on breaking even over roughly two seasons.

Selling, shelf life and FSSAI

Fresh mushrooms keep only about 7–10 days, so line up buyers before your first flush: sabzi vendors, restaurants, hostels and caterers. Pack in punnets with cling film and deliver within a day of harvest. If you have surplus oyster, dry it for powder or pickle, which keeps far longer. To sell packed food, small sellers usually need only basic FSSAI registration on FoSCoS. Confirm which category your turnover falls under there.

Subsidy: MIDH, NHB and state schemes

  • MIDH (revised 2025 guidelines): 40% assistance for private mushroom production and spawn units, and 50% for low-cost units, now in all districts. The subsidy is credit-linked and back-ended, which means a bank loan has to be sanctioned first. Caps differ by state: Uttarakhand reportedly caps it at ₹20 lakh for production units, and Rajasthan at ₹6 lakh on a ₹15 lakh project. Check current rates with your state horticulture department.
  • NHB: apply through the NHB online application and follow its status on the track application page. For a ₹50,000 home unit, the paperwork usually isn't worth it. It starts to make sense once you scale up.
  • Women's groups: ATMA gives ₹25,000 per women's food-security group, so ask at your block office.
  • Andhra Pradesh Mushroom Mission: announced around 27 April 2026 with a reported ₹13,000 crore budget. As of 31 October 2026, coverage disagrees on whether it has actually launched. Exam-prep sites cite "1.62 lakh units, 40% subsidy", but when we checked on 31 October 2026 we could not confirm either figure from an official source.

Morel mushroom farming: why it's still risky

Morels, called gucchi in Kashmir, reportedly sell for ₹15,000–40,000/kg, according to 2026 news coverage. That price is why YouTube is full of "morel mushroom farming" videos. Here's the honest picture:

  • DMR Solan first fruited morels indoors in 2021. At the time, the director said it would take 2–3 more years to reach farmers. DMR's collaborations page lists a 2025 Morchella MoU, but no farmer protocol has been published.
  • SKUAST-Kashmir researchers led by Prof. Tariq Ahmad Sofi say they have standardised controlled cultivation. This was first reported on 16 April 2026, and early reports said 3 of 10 test strains fruited. So far this comes only from news reports. We found no university paper or press release.
  • Abroad, the Danish Morel Project reports 4.2 kg/m² over 22 weeks, but its method is patented and not public. China mostly grows morels in outdoor beds, not in rooms.
  • Kashmir's wild harvest was reportedly very poor this year, which keeps prices high. It also tempts people to sell fake "morel spawn kits".

Verdict: don't put your savings into morels in 2026. Learn on oyster, and wait until DMR or SKUAST releases a public protocol.

Frequently asked questions

Which mushroom is easiest to grow at home in India?

Oyster (dhingri) is the easiest. It grows on pasteurised wheat or paddy straw in polybags, tolerates roughly 20–30°C, and is ready to harvest about 3–4 weeks after spawning. Milky mushroom works well as a summer crop in hot regions. Button mushroom needs about 14–18°C and bought compost, so it's better as an upgrade once you've had some success with oyster.

Can I grow morel (gucchi) mushrooms at home?

Not realistically yet. ICAR-DMR Solan fruited morels indoors in 2021 but hasn't published a farmer protocol. SKUAST-Kashmir's 2026 cultivation claims come only from news reports. Successful methods abroad are patented or need outdoor beds. Be careful with anyone selling morel spawn kits online. Start with oyster and wait for an official protocol.

Do I need a licence to sell mushrooms grown at home?

If you sell packed mushrooms, you will usually need FSSAI registration. Small sellers generally need only basic registration, which you apply for online on the FoSCoS portal (foscos.fssai.gov.in). Check the turnover limits on FoSCoS before applying. Selling loose produce to a local vendor is simpler, but restaurants and retailers often ask for an FSSAI number anyway.

Is there a government subsidy for mushroom farming?

Yes. Under the revised 2025 MIDH guidelines, private mushroom production and spawn units get 40% assistance and low-cost units get 50%. The subsidy is credit-linked and back-ended, so a bank loan must be sanctioned first. State caps vary. Apply through the National Horticulture Board portal or your state horticulture department. Women's groups can also ask about ATMA support.

How much profit can a one-room home mushroom farm make?

By our working estimate, a 150-bag oyster room costs ₹35,000–65,000 to set up and about ₹5,000–8,500 per cycle to run. It yields 100–150 kg per cycle and earns roughly ₹5,000–13,000 profit per cycle after spoilage. With 5–6 cycles a year, it breaks even in about 8–12 months. Selling prices in your area matter most.

Where can I get mushroom farming training in India?

ICAR-DMR Solan runs paid courses listed on its training calendar at dmrsolan.res.in. Upcoming batches run from 18–20 November 2026 (women, Hindi, ₹4,500) to March 2027, with registration through a Google Form. Your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra and state horticulture or agriculture university also run shorter, often cheaper, courses.
