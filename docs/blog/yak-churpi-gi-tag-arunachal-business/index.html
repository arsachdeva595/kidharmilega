Yak Churpi GI Tag: What Arunachal's Tag Covers and How to Start a Churpi Business

Only Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi holds a GI tag: Application 809, Certificate 511, dated 3 October 2023. Here's what the tag covers, why the November 2025 headlines confused people, and what a churpi business in West Kameng or Tawang could cost, earn and need.

October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

The yak churpi GI tag belongs to one product only: Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi. The GI Registry granted it under Application No. 809, and Certificate No. 511 is dated 3 October 2023. The applicant was ICAR–National Research Centre on Yak in Dirang, West Kameng. Churpi from Sikkim, Ladakh, Darjeeling or Nepal is not covered. Some news sites reported a "new" GI in November 2025, but that was a re-announcement of the 2023 grant.

If you have roots in West Kameng or Tawang and ₹5–20 lakh in savings, yeh page tumhare liye hai. Below we cover the official facts, then whether a churpi business makes sense: food-grade churpi, hard churpi for the global dog-chew market, sourcing from Brokpa herders, setup costs, margins and how to put the GI on your label legally.

Most news pages skip these details. They come straight from the GI Registry record for Application 809:

ICAR calls it the first yak-milk product in India to get a GI. It was granted in October 2023 together with Khaw Tai (Khamti rice) and Tangsa textile. Dr Vijay Paul, Principal Scientist at NRC-Yak, handled the application. Dr Mihir Sarkar was the Director and Padma Shri Dr Rajani Kant was the GI facilitator. The legal basis is the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration & Protection) Act, 1999, which has been in force since 15 September 2003. Internationally, the GI is protected under the Paris Convention and TRIPS Articles 22–24.

2023 or 2025? Clearing up the date confusion

On 24–25 November 2025, PTI, Arunachal Times and others ran "Yak churpi gets GI tag" stories after Deputy CM Chowna Mein posted about it on X. Those stories cite "Reg No 809", but 809 is the application number. The certificate is No. 511 from October 2023. Nothing new was granted in 2025.

Why is everyone searching this in October 2026?

We found no new GI decision in October 2026. The likely triggers below are our reading of the news, not confirmed causes:

  • Sikkim's first Himalayan Yak Festival in Gnathang Valley, around 27–29 October 2026. The Sikkim Livestock Development Board showed commercial yak churpi and A2 Siri Ghee prototypes, and ICAR-NBAGR registered the Sikkimese Yak as a breed, reports Assam Tribune. Sikkim has about 5,300 yaks, and India has about 58,000.
  • Ladakh churpi winning gold. Nordic Farm, Leh won gold at the Mundial do Queijo do Brasil 2026 for "Yak Churpi-Soft", covered by ThePrint.
  • Exam prep. UPSC and state-PSC current-affairs pages keep repeating this fact.

Does Sikkim, Ladakh or Darjeeling churpi have a GI tag?

No. We found no GI application for churpi from Ladakh, Sikkim or Darjeeling. Nepal's churpi isn't protected by an Indian GI either. If you make churpi in Leh or Gangtok, you can't use "Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi" or the GI logo, however good your cheese is. The tag covers churpi made from Arunachali yak milk, mainly in West Kameng and Tawang.

What exactly is yak churpi? Soft vs hard

Churpi is a naturally fermented, casein-based, cottage-cheese-like product made from skimmed yak milk. Brokpa pastoralists, and Monpa communities as well, move their yaks to about 10,000 ft or higher in summer and bring them down to mid-altitudes in winter. Churpi has been their protein source for centuries.

  • Soft churpi: fresh and crumbly. Locals cook it in curries in place of vegetables and eat it with rice. It has a short shelf life.
  • Hard churpi: pressed, dried and often smoked until it is rock-hard. According to NABARD's GI page, it can keep for up to about 20 years. This is the type that matters for the dog-chew trade.

It is protein-rich and contains probiotic lactic acid bacteria.

The business opportunity: three models

1. Food-grade GI churpi (domestic premium)

You sell soft and hard churpi with the GI on the label to Northeast diaspora stores, gourmet cheese shops in metros and tourists in Tawang and Dirang. For a price reference, ThePrint reports that Ladakh churpi now sells for ₹1,600–2,000/kg, up from about ₹600/kg. That product has no GI, so a GI-tagged Arunachal churpi with good branding could aim for a similar price band.

2. Hard churpi for the dog-chew market (export)

Hard "Himalayan dog chews" are a well-known category in the US and Europe, and most of the supply currently comes from Nepal. Note this: dog-chew churpi is not covered by the GI. The GI is registered under Class 29 food stuffs and specifies yak milk, while many commercial chews mix in cow milk. If you want to use the GI on a pet product, first ask the GI Registry and NRC-Yak in writing whether that is allowed. Until they say yes, sell the chew on its own brand and use phrases like "made in the Himalayas of Arunachal".

3. Aggregator for herders

This model needs the least capital. You buy hard churpi from Brokpa herders, grade it, test it, pack it and sell it in bulk to brands and exporters. You aren't making any cheese yourself. Your margin comes from consistent quality and reliable logistics.

Who should start this, and who should skip it

Right for you if: you have family or community ties in West Kameng or Tawang, you can spend summers near the high pastures, and you can wait 18–30 months to break even.

Skip if: you are based in the plains with no herder network, you need cash flow within 6 months, or you are relying only on Amazon US dog-chew sales. Export compliance and Nepal's price competition will hurt a first-time founder.

Margins: paise kaise aayenge

Here is an illustrative example. Plug in your own numbers once you have herder prices:

  • Hard churpi bought from herders (assumption): ₹800/kg
  • Grading, drying losses, packaging and transport (assumption): ₹250–350/kg
  • Landed cost: about ₹1,050–1,150/kg
  • Selling at ₹1,600/kg (the lower end of ThePrint's Ladakh price band) gives a gross margin of roughly 28–34%

Retail sales in 100–250 g packs can earn more per kg, but marketing costs are higher too. For dog-chew exports, check what Himalayan chews currently sell for wholesale and on Chewy or Amazon listings before you fix a price. Don't assume retail prices abroad turn into your factory-gate price.

Sourcing from Brokpa herders: do it right

  • Seasonality: herds are high up in summer and come down in winter. Plan to buy stock in bulk when the herds are at mid-altitudes.
  • Fair, written prices: India's yak numbers are falling. A buyer who pays on time and pays fairly helps keep herding families in the trade, and buyers like that are rare.
  • Traceability: record the herder, village, district and batch date. Buyers ask for this, and a GI claim depends on it.
  • Purity: agree in writing that the churpi is 100% yak milk. Even a little mixed cow milk can break a GI claim.

How to use the yak churpi GI tag on your labels

  1. Produce inside the GI area and follow the method described in the registered specification. You can read it in the GI Registry public search under Application 809.
  2. Apply as an Authorised User of the GI through IP India, which runs the GI Registry in Chennai. Ask NRC-Yak, Dirang (the registered proprietor) for support. Your application must link back to the registered GI.
  3. Use the exact GI name, "Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi". Add the GI registration details and the official Indian GI logo only after you are registered as an Authorised User.
  4. Keep batch records so you can prove origin if anyone challenges your claim.
  5. Renewal: the GI is valid until 27 December 2031. Note that date in your calendar, because buyers will ask about it.

Schemes that can fund a churpi unit

  • PMFME: a 35% credit-linked capital subsidy for micro food-processing units, capped at ₹10 lakh. ODOP products get preference. West Kameng's ODOP is apple and Tawang's is chillies, so ask the Arunachal State Nodal Agency whether a churpi unit qualifies.
  • PMEGP: a margin-money subsidy on new micro units, routed through KVIC/DIC and banks. Hill and NE-region applicants get the higher subsidy slab.
  • Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund (AHIDF): interest subvention for dairy processing infrastructure. It fits better if your unit grows larger.
  • NABARD: it supports GI products and FPOs. A herder FPO can be your sourcing partner and a co-applicant.

Launch roadmap: step by step

  1. Month 0–1: visit NRC-Yak, Dirang. Read the GI specification and meet 10–15 herder families in West Kameng and Tawang.
  2. Month 1–2: run a trial purchase of 50–100 kg of hard churpi. Get moisture and microbial tests done at an accredited lab.
  3. Month 2–3: get FSSAI registration (basic registration if turnover is up to ₹12 lakh) and Udyam registration. File a trademark for your brand.
  4. Month 3–4: apply as a GI Authorised User. Submit the PMFME or PMEGP loan file.
  5. Month 4–6: set up the drying and packing unit. Pilot sales in Tawang tourist shops, Guwahati, Delhi NE stores and online.
  6. Month 6–12: if you plan to export, get an IEC from DGFT. Check APEDA registration for dairy products and the veterinary health certificate rules for your target country. Pet food going to the US and EU needs facility registration or approval there.
  7. Month 12+: sign one bulk buyer or importer before you scale up.

Risks to price in

  • Nepal dominates the dog-chew market on price, so compete on traceability and quality, not on cost.
  • Yak numbers are falling, so raw material supply is limited.
  • Roads to high-altitude areas close in winter.
  • Food-safety and export documents take time. Budget 3–6 months for them.

Frequently asked questions

When did yak churpi get the GI tag?

Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi was registered as a GI with Certificate No. 511, dated 3 October 2023. The application (No. 809) was filed on 28 December 2021 by ICAR-National Research Centre on Yak, Dirang. Some news reports from November 2025 said it had just received a GI, but they were repeating the 2023 grant after the Deputy CM posted about it.

Does Sikkim or Ladakh churpi have a GI tag?

No. Only Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi, made mainly in West Kameng and Tawang, has a GI tag. We found no GI application for churpi from Sikkim, Ladakh or Darjeeling. Ladakh's Nordic Farm won gold in Brazil in 2026, and Sikkim held its first Yak Festival in October 2026, but neither product is protected by a GI.

Are churpi dog chews covered by the yak churpi GI?

Not automatically. The GI is registered under Class 29 food stuffs for churpi made from Arunachali yak milk. Many dog chews sold abroad mix in cow milk and come from Nepal. If you want to use the GI on a pet product, first get written clarification from the GI Registry and NRC-Yak, the registered proprietor.

How can a producer use the GI tag on churpi packaging?

Produce churpi inside the GI area following the registered specification, then apply to IP India's GI Registry as an Authorised User of Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi. Once you are registered, you can use the GI name and the official Indian GI logo on your packs. Keep batch-wise records of herders and villages so you can prove origin.

How much does it cost to start a churpi business?

Our planning estimate for a small hard-churpi drying and packing unit is about ₹8.5–17.5 lakh, including ₹3–6 lakh of working capital. An aggregator model, where you buy, grade and pack churpi from herders, can start at ₹3–5 lakh. These are not supplier quotes, so get local quotations and check PMFME or PMEGP subsidies before you borrow.

How long is the yak churpi GI tag valid?

The GI registration for Arunachal Pradesh Yak Churpi is valid until 27 December 2031, which is 10 years from the filing date of 28 December 2021. Under the GI Act, 1999, it can be renewed for further 10-year periods, so the protection can continue indefinitely as long as it is renewed on time.
