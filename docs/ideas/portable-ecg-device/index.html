Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • India imports most of its medical devices, and the government is pushing local manufacturing through production incentives and device parks.
  • Home care, diagnostics and monitoring are growing as hospitals discharge patients sooner.
  • Grants from BIRAC, state startup missions and incubators at IITs and medical colleges fund early prototypes.
  • Indian devices priced for Indian hospitals can also sell in Africa, South-East Asia and the Middle East.

Headwinds and traps

  • Every medical device now needs a CDSCO licence, and higher-risk devices need clinical data.
  • Hospitals are slow to try new devices and prefer known brands.
  • Government tenders favour the lowest price and pay late.
  • Development takes years, and most founders underestimate the time to first revenue.

What changed in 2026

India now regulates every medical device, which raises the cost of entry but also makes a certified Indian device more credible. In 2026 the startups that get traction solve a problem specific to Indian care, such as low-cost screening, home monitoring or rural clinics, and plan for certification from day one.

Portable ECGs sell to GPs, pharmacies, rural clinics and health-conscious families. Accuracy must match hospital machines, and CDSCO approval is required. Recurring revenue from reports or cardiologist review adds value.

What investors grill founders on in this category

  • What class is the device under CDSCO rules, and where are you in the licence process?
  • What clinical evidence do you have, and from which hospitals?
  • Who pays: the patient, the hospital, an insurer or the government?
  • What does each device earn after the sale, from consumables or service?
  • How long is the sales cycle for a hospital?

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Join a medical-technology incubator, apply for BIRAC and state grants, and run a pilot with one hospital before raising equity. Grants and pilot data together make the first funding round far easier.

Licences and government support

Medical device parks and incubators in places such as Visakhapatnam (AMTZ), Hyderabad and Bengaluru offer shared testing labs, which cuts the cost of certification significantly.

Licences you'll need

  • CDSCO manufacturing licence under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 (class decides the process)
  • ISO 13485 quality management certification
  • Electrical safety and other testing from accredited labs
  • Ethics committee approval for any clinical study
  • Company registration, GST and Udyam

Government support worth checking

  • Startup India (DPIIT recognition): Recognised startups get easier compliance, tax-holiday eligibility and access to government seed and fund-of-funds programmes.
  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.
  • Stand-Up India: Bank loans from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for new businesses started by women or SC/ST founders.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • It takes years

    From prototype to certified product usually takes 2–4 years. Plan your funding for the long route, not the optimistic one.

  • Doctors must believe it

    No hospital will buy a device doctors don't trust. You need clinical champions, published data and patience.

  • Tenders are a trap for small firms

    Government tenders buy the cheapest certified option and can pay months late. Treat them as a later channel, not the first one.

  • Service is part of the product

    A device that breaks without quick service gets you blacklisted. Budget for training, service engineers and spare parts.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is a working prototype, a signed pilot with one hospital and a clear route to your CDSCO licence.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate the problem

    • Spend time in 3–5 hospitals or clinics watching the problem you want to solve.
    • Interview 20 doctors, nurses and buyers about what they use today and what they pay.
    • Check how CDSCO classifies similar devices.

  2. Days 16–45

    Prototype and plan

    • Build a rough prototype you can show doctors.
    • Apply to a medical-technology incubator and for a BIRAC or state grant.
    • Map the tests and certifications you'll need, with costs.

  3. Days 46–75

    Find a clinical partner

    • Sign a pilot or study agreement with one hospital.
    • Start the ethics committee process.
    • Register the company and file a provisional patent if there's something new.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review

    • Collect doctor feedback on the prototype.
    • Estimate cost per unit at 1,000 units.
    • Decide whether to pursue grants, a pilot sale or an angel round next.
