Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Mandatory BIS rules for helmets and other safety products push out low-quality imports.
  • Companies, factories and fleets must meet safety rules.
  • Smart devices with alerts and tracking add value.
  • Families pay for personal safety and elder-care devices.

Headwinds and traps

  • Certification takes time and money.
  • Cheap, uncertified products still sell widely.
  • Institutions buy slowly and through tenders.
  • Device failures can have serious consequences and liability.

What changed in 2026

Safety products are increasingly regulated, which favours certified Indian makers. In 2026, safety device businesses grow by selling to institutions that must comply, such as factories, fleets and schools, and adding smart features that make safety measurable.

Automatic extinguishers activate without human action. Homes, shops and vehicles are buyers. BIS certification and demonstrations build trust.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Certify one product, win a few institutional buyers with demonstrations, and use their references to reach more.

Licences and government support

Licences you'll need

  • BIS certification where mandatory (for example, helmets and fire extinguishers)
  • GST registration
  • Udyam registration
  • Product liability insurance
  • Trademark application

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.
  • Startup India (DPIIT recognition): Recognised startups get easier compliance, tax-holiday eligibility and access to government seed and fund-of-funds programmes.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Certification delays

    Plan for months of testing.

  • Liability

    If a device fails, consequences can be serious. Insure and test thoroughly.

  • Cheap competitors

    Uncertified products undercut you. Sell to buyers who need compliance.

  • Slow institutional sales

    Tenders and approvals take time.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is a certified product and first orders from 3 institutional buyers.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate

    • Talk to 20 buyers about safety problems.
    • Check certification requirements.
    • Plan the product.

  2. Days 16–45

    Develop

    • Build and test prototypes.
    • Start certification.
    • Line up a manufacturer.

  3. Days 46–75

    Demonstrate

    • Demo to 10 institutions.
    • Offer pilots.
    • Collect feedback.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review

    • Secure first orders.
    • Calculate margins.
    • Plan dealer network.
