Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Drones, robotics, space and defence are priority sectors with government support and liberalised rules.
  • Grants from DST, iDEX (defence), BIRAC and state programmes fund early research.
  • IIT and university incubators offer labs and mentors.
  • Large companies and governments are looking for Indian suppliers instead of imports.

Headwinds and traps

  • Hardware takes years and a lot of money to develop.
  • Buyers such as governments and defence take a long time to decide.
  • Certification and testing can take months.
  • Talent in robotics, electronics and AI is expensive and scarce.

What changed in 2026

India's drone rules, defence procurement from startups through iDEX, and the space sector reforms have opened markets that were closed a few years ago. In 2026, the deeptech startups that succeed pick one buyer with a painful, expensive problem and use grants and paid pilots to fund development.

Drones are used for crop spraying, surveys, inspections and filming. The Drone Rules, 2021 and production incentives support Indian makers. Training pilots and servicing drones add revenue.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Win grants and a paid pilot before raising equity. A paying customer and a working prototype make the first funding round much easier.

Licences and government support

Licences you'll need

  • Company registration and DPIIT startup recognition
  • Drone registration and type certification under the Drone Rules, 2021, for drones
  • Import licences for restricted components
  • Patent filings
  • Industry-specific safety certifications

Government support worth checking

  • Startup India (DPIIT recognition): Recognised startups get easier compliance, tax-holiday eligibility and access to government seed and fund-of-funds programmes.
  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • It takes years

    Most hardware startups take 3–5 years to reach steady revenue. Plan your funding for that.

  • Pilots don't always convert

    Many pilots end without a purchase order. Ask for paid pilots with clear success criteria.

  • Talent is scarce

    Good engineers are in demand. Equity, mission and interesting problems help you compete.

  • Scaling production is hard

    Making ten units is very different from making a thousand. Plan manufacturing early.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is a working prototype, one grant application and a signed pilot with a paying buyer.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate the problem

    • Talk to 20 potential buyers about the problem and what it costs them.
    • Map which grants and programmes fit.
    • Join an incubator.

  2. Days 16–45

    Prototype

    • Build a rough prototype.
    • Apply for grants.
    • File a provisional patent if there's something new.

  3. Days 46–75

    Pilot

    • Demonstrate to 5 buyers.
    • Negotiate one paid pilot.
    • Plan certification and testing.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review

    • Review pilot feedback.
    • Estimate cost at scale.
    • Decide on grants, angel funding or more pilots.
