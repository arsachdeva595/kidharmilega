Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Parents spend heavily on education, even in smaller towns.
  • Cheap smartphones and data let students learn online anywhere.
  • AI makes personalised learning and content creation cheaper.
  • The National Education Policy pushes skills, coding and early learning.

Headwinds and traps

  • Byju's collapse and aggressive sales tactics damaged trust in edtech.
  • Free content on YouTube competes with paid courses.
  • Student engagement drops quickly after sign-up.
  • Schools buy slowly and pay late.

What changed in 2026

After the edtech boom and bust, parents and schools are wary of big promises. In 2026, edtech startups that grow focus on one clear outcome, like reading, spoken English or a specific exam, prove results, and sell honestly instead of pushing loans and long contracts.

Colleges have many clubs and events but no good way to share them. Brands and recruiters pay to reach students. College ambassador programmes drive growth.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with live classes for a small group, prove results, then turn what works into recorded content and software.

Licences and government support

Licences you'll need

  • Company or LLP registration
  • GST registration
  • Privacy policy and parental consent for children's data under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act
  • Compliance with the consumer protection guidelines on online education

Government support worth checking

  • Startup India (DPIIT recognition): Recognised startups get easier compliance, tax-holiday eligibility and access to government seed and fund-of-funds programmes.
  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Trust is low

    After edtech scandals, parents are sceptical. Offer free trials and honest refund policies.

  • Engagement drops

    Most students stop after the first few weeks. Live elements and progress tracking help.

  • Free content

    YouTube offers much for free. You need structure, feedback and results.

  • Schools are slow

    School sales take months. Plan for long cycles.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is 100 paying students, with at least 60% completing.

  1. Days 1–15

    Pick one outcome

    • Choose one age group and one result.
    • Talk to 30 parents or students.
    • Check competing courses.

  2. Days 16–45

    Pilot

    • Run a live pilot with 10–20 students.
    • Measure progress.
    • Refine content.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell

    • Launch paid classes.
    • Partner with 3 schools or coaching centres.
    • Collect testimonials.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Track completion and renewals.
    • Calculate cost per student.
    • Decide what to automate.
