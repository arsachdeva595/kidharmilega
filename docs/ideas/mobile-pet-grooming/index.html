Should You Start a Mobile Pet Grooming Business in 2026?

An honest reality check and setup guide: what it costs, what you keep, what Shark Tank India taught founders in this space, and a 90-day plan to test it.

The 30-second verdict

Yes, if you start with home visits and buy a van only once your calendar is full. Pet owners in Indian cities pay well for doorstep grooming and rebook every four to six weeks. The van is where most founders go wrong: its monthly loan payment only pays off at four to six grooms a day, and traffic makes that hard.

Initial capital

₹1.5–3 lakh

Scaled: ₹12–25 lakh for a fitted grooming van with water tank, power and two groomers

30–45% operating margin per groom, before van loan payments

Groomer pay and consumables are 35–45% of each booking

Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • Pet ownership has grown fast in Indian cities, and owners increasingly treat pets as family.
  • Busy owners pay extra for doorstep services instead of taking an anxious pet to a salon.
  • Pet parents are loyal once they trust a groomer, which makes repeat bookings likely every 4–6 weeks.
  • Vets, pet stores and apartment communities are easy referral partners.

Headwinds and traps

  • Skilled groomers are scarce and often leave to start on their own.
  • Traffic limits a van to a handful of appointments a day in big cities.
  • Parking, water and wastewater disposal at each stop can be a daily headache.
  • Anxious or aggressive pets bring injury risk to the pet and the groomer.

What changed in 2026

Pet services have moved from a luxury to a routine expense for many urban owners, and booking apps have made doorstep grooming normal. That also means more competition, so in 2026 the grooming businesses that last compete on trust: the same groomer every time, gentle handling and clear hygiene standards.

Doorstep grooming suits anxious pets and busy owners, and apartment communities spread recommendations quickly. Shark Tank India saw a mobile pet grooming pitch in Season 1 (Flying Fur), and the questions were familiar: how many grooms a van does per day, and how you keep trained groomers. A founder who builds trust with a few hundred regular clients through vets and housing societies has a steady, cash-positive local business.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Start with home visits using a portable kit, build a list of 100–150 regular clients through vets and apartment groups, and buy a van only when your calendar is full most weeks.

Licences and government support

Buy shampoos, blades and consumables from pet wholesale suppliers in bulk; professional-grade supplies cost much less per groom than retail packs.

Licences you'll need

  • Trade licence or Shops and Establishments registration
  • Commercial registration and permits for a grooming van
  • GST registration once you cross the turnover threshold
  • Udyam registration
  • Liability insurance covering the pets in your care

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • Stand-Up India: Bank loans from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for new businesses started by women or SC/ST founders.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Travel eats your day

    In a big city, driving between appointments can take as long as the grooms themselves. Group bookings by area and day of the week, or your income stalls at three grooms a day.

  • Groomers leave

    A trained groomer can start their own service with a few thousand rupees of equipment. Pay well, share bookings fairly, and make sure clients know the business, not just the person.

  • Cancellations

    Last-minute cancellations leave empty slots you can't fill. Take a small advance on booking and keep a waiting list.

  • The van loan

    A van EMI of ₹25,000–40,000 a month is fixed whether it's busy or not. Buying a van before demand is proven is the most common way this business fails.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹1 lakh in bookings and a list of repeat clients before you buy a van.

  1. Days 1–15

    Validate and prepare

    • Complete a grooming course or hire a certified groomer.
    • Talk to 30 pet owners and 5 vets about what they pay and what worries them.
    • Price your services against 3 salons and 2 apps nearby.

  2. Days 16–45

    Launch home visits

    • Buy a portable kit and get liability insurance.
    • Set up a Google Business profile and a simple booking form.
    • Do 20 discounted first grooms for friends' and neighbours' pets.

  3. Days 46–75

    Build referrals

    • Leave cards with 5 vets and 3 pet stores and offer them a referral fee.
    • Post before-and-after photos (with owners' permission) in apartment groups.
    • Offer a discount for booking the next groom on the spot.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Count grooms per day, rebooking rate and income per hour including travel.
    • Decide whether to hire a second groomer or add a van.
    • Raise prices for difficult breeds and long coats.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start mobile pet grooming?

Home visits with a portable kit cost ₹1.5–3 lakh including training and insurance. A fitted grooming van with water, heating and power costs ₹12–25 lakh.

How much can a pet groomer earn per groom?

A full groom at home sells for ₹800–2,500 depending on the breed and coat. After consumables, travel and groomer pay, ₹450–700 per groom is typical before any van loan.

Do I need a certificate to groom pets?

There is no mandatory licence, but a professional grooming course and supervised practice are essential for safe handling and for earning owners' trust.

How many grooms can a mobile van do in a day?

In big-city traffic, four to six grooms a day is realistic if you group appointments by area.
