Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • India has deep garment clusters such as Tiruppur, Ludhiana, Surat and Jaipur that make small runs possible.
  • Instagram, creators and quick-commerce fashion have made it cheaper to reach a niche audience.
  • Shark Tank India funded more apparel brands than almost any other category, from innerwear to streetwear.
  • Buyers in tier-2 and tier-3 cities now shop online for branded clothing, not just metros.

Headwinds and traps

  • Return rates of 20–35% are common for online apparel, driven by size and fit.
  • Meta and Google ad costs keep rising, and many brands spend 25–40% of revenue on ads.
  • Fast-fashion players like Myntra's own labels, Zudio and Shein-backed brands price very aggressively.
  • Unsold sizes and colours turn into dead stock that you clear at a loss.

What changed in 2026

Generic basics are a price war you can't win against Zudio and marketplace labels. The apparel brands that grew on Shark Tank each solved one specific problem for one specific buyer: innerwear for teenage girls, shapewear, modest fashion, army-inspired designs. In 2026, a narrow customer and a fit that works are worth more than a big catalogue.

Heat- and UV-reactive inks make T-shirts change colour with sunlight or touch, which appeals to children and gift buyers. The key risks are durability after washing and the product being seen as a one-time novelty. Test wash durability carefully and consider school, event and corporate orders.

What you keep10–20% after ads and returns; more on repeat customers

Repeat customers cost almost nothing in ads. Brands that get 30%+ of orders from repeat buyers can afford to grow; brands that rely on new customers every month rarely do.

What investors grill founders on in this category

  • What is your return rate, and how much of it is size-related?
  • How much do you spend on ads for each first order?
  • What share of revenue comes from repeat customers?
  • How much stock is sitting unsold, and how old is it?
  • Why would someone buy from you instead of a marketplace label at half the price?

These are the recurring questions in this business model, not quotes from the episodes above.

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Launch with a small range in one niche, produce in batches of 50–100 per design, and reorder only what sells at full price. Pre-orders and drops keep stock low while you learn your sizes.

Garment clusters such as Tiruppur (knitwear), Ludhiana (woollens), Surat (synthetics), Jaipur (prints) and Kolkata (innerwear) will take smaller orders and move faster than big exporters. Visiting in person gets you better prices and more honest timelines.

Licences you'll need

  • GST registration (needed for marketplaces)
  • Udyam registration
  • Trademark application for your brand name
  • Shops and Establishments registration if you run an office or warehouse
  • Correct labelling on each garment: fibre content, size, care and maker details

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.
  • Stand-Up India: Bank loans from ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for new businesses started by women or SC/ST founders.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Returns eat your profit

    Every return costs shipping both ways, repacking and sometimes a damaged piece. A brand with 30% returns needs a much higher price just to break even. Invest in size charts, fit notes and exchanges instead of refunds.

  • You rent your customers from Meta

    If most sales come from paid ads, a rise in ad costs can wipe out your margin overnight. Build an email, WhatsApp and community list from day one.

  • Dead stock

    The sizes and colours that don't sell tie up cash. Brands that run out of S and M while holding XXL are common. Reorder in small batches and track sell-through by size.

  • Copying is instant

    A successful design can appear on marketplaces within weeks at a lower price. Your protection is the brand, the fit and the community, not the design itself.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹2 lakh in sales with at least 20% from repeat buyers, and a return rate below 20%.

  1. Days 1–15

    Pick your niche and sample

    • Define one buyer and one problem your clothing solves for them.
    • Visit a cluster or contact 5 manufacturers and get quotes for 50–100 pieces per design.
    • Make samples of 4–6 designs and try them on real people of different sizes.

  2. Days 16–45

    Small first run

    • Register for GST and Udyam and apply for your trademark.
    • Produce a small run of the best 3–4 designs.
    • Shoot content that shows fit on different body types.

  3. Days 46–75

    Sell and learn

    • Launch on Instagram with 10–20 creators in your niche.
    • Test small ad budgets on 2–3 designs.
    • Call every buyer who returns a piece and ask why.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Check ad cost per order, returns by size and repeat orders.
    • Reorder the designs that sold at full price.
    • Decide whether to add marketplaces or stay direct.

What licences and registrations do I need?

GST registration (needed for marketplaces); Udyam registration; Trademark application for your brand name; Shops and Establishments registration if you run an office or warehouse.

What profit margin can I expect?

55–70% gross margin on your own site, before returns and ads; 25–35% on marketplaces after commissions and returns. After all costs, expect to keep 10–20% after ads and returns; more on repeat customers.

How long does it take to break even?

Usually 12–24 months for a small setup, if you control costs and build repeat customers early.
