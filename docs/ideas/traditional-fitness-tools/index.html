Market reality 2026: why now, or why not now?

Tailwinds

  • India's sports clusters, Jalandhar and Meerut in particular, make most of the country's sports equipment and can produce small batches.
  • New sports such as pickleball, padel and trekking are growing quickly in cities.
  • Fitness, yoga and outdoor activity are now year-round habits for many urban buyers.
  • Khelo India, school sports programmes and new academies are adding buyers in smaller towns.

Headwinds and traps

  • Decathlon sells good-quality basics at very low prices.
  • Cheap imports dominate many categories, from yoga mats to rackets.
  • Serious players trust established international brands for bats, shoes and rackets.
  • Many products are seasonal or tied to one sport's popularity.

What changed in 2026

Decathlon has made basic sports gear cheap, so a generic brand struggles. What works in 2026 is depth in one sport: gear designed with coaches for Indian conditions, a new sport that is growing before big brands arrive, or a craft product such as Kashmir willow bats with a clear origin.

Traditional Indian training tools such as the mudgar and gada are getting new attention from fitness creators and functional-training gyms. They are simple to make but heavy to ship. Content that teaches how to use them safely is what turns interest into sales.

What investors grill founders on in this category

  • Why would a serious player choose you over an established brand?
  • What share of sales comes through coaches and academies?
  • How seasonal are your sales?
  • How do you control quality across batches?

The takeaway for a 2026 bootstrapper

Win one community first. Give gear to 10–20 coaches and players who others follow, build your reputation there, and expand to a second sport only after the first sells on referrals.

The cluster advantage: local sourcing and ODOP playbook

These districts have ODOP products that feed this business model.

  • Jalandhar, Punjab — Sports Goods · setup ₹1.5L–₹25L, D2C margin 50-65%
  • Meerut, Uttar Pradesh — Sports Products · setup ₹5L–₹50L, D2C margin 50-65%

Jalandhar and Meerut make most of India's balls, bats, rackets and fitness gear, and Kashmir's willow clusters make cricket bats. Buying directly from these makers, instead of through city traders, cuts costs and lets you control specifications.

Licences you'll need

  • GST registration
  • Udyam registration
  • Trademark application
  • BIS or safety standards where they apply (for example, helmets and some fitness equipment)
  • Import-export code if you export

Government support worth checking

  • Udyam registration: Free, online, Aadhaar-based MSME registration. Most MSME benefits, including priority-sector bank loans, start here.
  • PM Mudra Yojana: Collateral-free bank loans in four tiers: Shishu (up to ₹50,000), Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh), Tarun (up to ₹10 lakh) and Tarun Plus (up to ₹20 lakh, for borrowers who repaid a Tarun loan).
  • PMEGP: Credit-linked subsidy for new units: projects up to ₹50 lakh (manufacturing) or ₹20 lakh (services), with 15–35% of project cost as subsidy depending on your category and whether you are in a rural area.
  • CGTMSE: A government credit guarantee that lets banks lend to micro and small enterprises without collateral. Ask your bank for a CGTMSE-covered loan.

Scheme limits change. Confirm current terms on the official portal or with your bank before applying.

The hard truths: why most attempts fail in year one

  • Trust is earned on the field

    Players try gear because someone they respect uses it. Ads alone rarely convince serious players.

  • Decathlon sets the price

    For any basic product, buyers will compare you with Decathlon. Compete on features, fit or sport-specific design, not price.

  • Trends fade

    New sports grow fast and then level off. Don't buy stock for next year's boom.

  • Quality complaints spread

    A bat that breaks or a paddle that chips is discussed in every club. One bad batch can undo years of reputation.

The 90-day execution blueprint

The aim is ₹1.5 lakh in sales with at least 5 coaches or academies buying again.

  1. Days 1–15

    Pick your sport

    • Choose one sport and one type of player.
    • Interview 10 coaches and 20 players about what they use and what frustrates them.
    • Visit or contact 3 makers in a sports cluster.

  2. Days 16–45

    Sample and test

    • Make samples and have players test them for 2–3 weeks.
    • Fix what they complain about.
    • Register your brand and set up a simple store.

  3. Days 46–75

    Launch through players

    • Give gear to 10–20 coaches and players with a following.
    • Set up an academy discount or referral scheme.
    • Show up at tournaments and club events.

  4. Days 76–90

    Review the numbers

    • Check margins after shipping and returns.
    • See which coaches reorder and why.
    • Decide on the next product or the next city.
