opportunity, Parbhani mein.

Parbhani district has ideal agro-climatic conditions (black soil, 700-800mm rainfall) for gram cultivation. The district produces over 2.5 lakh tonnes of grams annually, making it one of Maharashtra's top gram-growing regions. Well-established supply chains and farmer networks ensure consistent raw material availability at competitive prices.

Step-by-Step Roadmap

Zero se pehli sale tak — actual kaam

Har step realistic hai — Parbhani ke liye, as is.

1
Learn / Research
Visit Parbhani gram mandi (main agricultural market) and connect with 5+ large traders. Study pricing at Nanded-Parbhani corridor. Attend Maharashtra Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) workshops. Network with FPOs in Parbhani district.
2
Source Raw Materials
Source 10-15 MT first batch from licensed APMC traders in Parbhani mandi at ₹5,500-6,500/MT. Budget ₹75-100L for first inventory including transport, cleaning, and packaging. Visit farms in Sonepat, Pathri blocks to negotiate farmer-direct contracts for future batches.
3
Produce / Partner
Establish small processing unit (cleaning, grading, packaging) in Parbhani industrial area or partner with existing dal millers (Shivaji Chakki, Arjun Chakki). Install storage capacity of 50-100 MT with proper moisture control. Obtain Food License (FSSAI) from Parbhani Municipal Corporation.
4
Brand & Register
Register under Udyam portal as small manufacturer. Obtain IEC (Importer-Exporter Code) from DGFT. Register brand name with Trademark Office. Apply for FSSAI food business license (₹2,000-5,000). Certify for organic if applicable (NPOP certification for export).
5
First Sale
Launch on Amazon, Flipkart with ₹1,500-2,500/kg pricing. Create WhatsApp bulk order group targeting restaurants in Pune, Mumbai. Approach HOAR (Hotel & Restaurant Association) members. Register with Tradepathmark and Globaltrade for B2B export inquiries. Start with 2-3 key institutional customers (NGOs, schools).
6
Scale
Secure PMMY: Shishu up to ₹50,000; Kishore up to ₹5 lakh; Tarun up to ₹10 lakh; Tarun Plus up to ₹20 lakh (maximum ₹20 lakh). The bank sets the interest rate. From 1 April 2026, MSE loans are collateral-free up to ₹20 lakh under RBI/2025-26/206; banks may go up to ₹25 lakh based on track record. Build direct contracts with 20+ farmers for supply consistency. Launch value-added products (gram flour, sprouts). Set up B2B export orders via APEDA-registered trading platforms. Expand to adjacent markets (Aurangabad, Nashik). Hire sales team for institutional sales.
Government Schemes

Woh paisa jo tujhe pata nahi tha

🏛
PMEGP (Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme)
New unit under the December 2023 guidelines: maximum project cost ₹50 lakh for manufacturing and ₹20 lakh for service or business. Margin-money subsidy is 15% of project cost in urban areas and 25% in rural areas for the general category, and 25% urban / 35% rural for special categories (SC, ST, OBC, minorities, women, ex-servicemen, transgender persons, persons with disability, NER, hill and border areas, and aspirational districts). Own contribution is 10% for general category and 5% for special categories. For MSE loans sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 April 2026, including PMEGP units, banks must lend without collateral up to ₹20 lakh under RBI's Lending to MSME Sector (Amendment) Directions, 2026 (RBI/2025-26/206, 9 February 2026). Banks may go up to ₹25 lakh without collateral based on the borrower's track record and good repayment, with CGTMSE cover. Apply at pmegp.msme.gov.in (PMEGP 2.0). The scheme was approved through 2025-26; treat any extension as unconfirmed until the Ministry of MSME publishes it.
🏛
PM Mudra Yojana (PMMY)
PMMY ceilings: Shishu up to ₹50,000; Kishore above ₹50,000 and up to ₹5 lakh; Tarun above ₹5 lakh and up to ₹10 lakh; Tarun Plus above ₹10 lakh and up to ₹20 lakh, and only after an earlier Tarun loan has been repaid. The maximum is ₹20 lakh. The lending bank sets the interest rate; the scheme does not fix one rate. From 1 April 2026, MSE loans are collateral-free up to ₹20 lakh under RBI's Lending to MSME Sector (Amendment) Directions, 2026 (RBI/2025-26/206, 9 February 2026); banks may go up to ₹25 lakh based on track record. The Tarun ceiling of ₹10 lakh is the product tier, not that collateral-free limit. Apply through a bank, NBFC or MFI, or at udyamimitra.in.
