Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme Explained: Incentives, Eligibility and Where Small Suppliers Fit

On 28 October 2026 the Cabinet approved a ₹10,000 crore Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS) running to FY 2039-40. This guide covers the funding split, the 1 lakh and 25,000 TEU thresholds, India's current container makers, and three realistic ways a first-time founder can get in.

October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

The Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS) is a ₹10,000 crore central scheme that the Union Cabinet approved on 28 October 2026. It pays support for every shipping container that is built in India to global standards and then sold, and it runs from FY 2027-28 to FY 2039-40. The scheme is designed for big plants. A new plant needs at least 1 lakh TEU of capacity a year, so if you have ₹2.5–20 lakh in savings, you can't build one. Your realistic way in is component supply, fabrication job-work or container refurbishing. Yeh page tumhare liye hai if you want the facts first and then an honest answer on where you fit.

About 89% of the money is tied to containers that are actually sold. This is an output-linked scheme, so a plant only gets paid when it delivers containers. The per-container subsidy rate hasn't been published yet. Be wary of anyone who quotes you an exact figure.

Who qualifies: CMPS eligibility thresholds

  • Greenfield (new) plant: at least 1 lakh TEU a year of capacity.
  • Brownfield (expansion): must add at least 25,000 TEU a year.
  • Local content: must meet the rising domestic value addition requirement, from 70% up to 90%.

Several reports link these capacity thresholds specifically to the interest subvention. Final eligibility for each component will only be clear once the guidelines are out. Either way, 25,000 TEU is factory-scale volume. According to The Week, an Inter-Ministerial Steering Committee chaired by the MoPSW Secretary (named as Vijay Kumar) will approve the guidelines and sanction assistance.

How to apply: there's no portal yet

This is the most important point on this page. As of 31 October 2026 there is no application portal, no application window and no published guidelines. The steering committee still has to approve them. If a consultant asks you for an "application fee" to file your CMPS claim, don't pay.

Here's what to watch:

  1. The MoPSW website (shipmin.gov.in), for the guidelines and notices.
  2. PIB releases from MoPSW. The MoPSW detail release is the current reference.
  3. Announcements on the per-container assistance rate and the testing-facility locations. These matter most for small suppliers.

CMAS to CMPS: how we got here

  • 1 February 2026: The Union Budget 2026-27 announced a ₹10,000 crore container scheme "over five years". Early material called it the Container Manufacturing Assistance Scheme (CMAS).
  • 17 March 2026: A Rajya Sabha reply said the scheme was still being appraised.
  • 3 July 2026: Sonowal unveiled India's first India-made EXIM container at the Maersk–CONCOR depot in Dadri, UP. It was built by DCM Shriram to ISO 1496 and CSC standards, and Maersk placed an initial order for 1,000 containers.
  • August 2026: PIB published a backgrounder.
  • 28 October 2026: The Cabinet approved it, renamed it CMPS and set the timeline as FY 2027-28 to FY 2039-40.

CMAS and CMPS are the same scheme under a new name. Use the Cabinet timeline, because older "five-year" figures are out of date.

Container manufacturing companies in India

If you searched for a container manufacturing company in India, whether as an investor or as a possible supplier, these are the names that come up most:

  • DCM Shriram / DCM Containers (formerly DCM Hyundai): made India's first EXIM container and holds the Maersk order.
  • Kalyani Cast-Tech: ISO and specialised containers, plus corner castings, the steel corner blocks that let containers be locked and stacked.
  • Jupiter Wagons: ISO marine and specialised containers, including energy-storage containers.
  • Adani Ports (APSEZ): reported in May 2026 to be evaluating container manufacturing. Nothing beyond that is confirmed.
  • Indirect beneficiaries: steelmakers such as Tata Steel, JSW Steel and SAIL, which can supply steel.

CONCOR buys and operates containers but doesn't make them. For suppliers, it's a buyer to keep track of, not a manufacturer.

Why India imports containers, and the China problem

China makes most of the world's containers. Estimates range from 85% to 96%, and the ministry cites about 95%. CIMC is the largest maker. India's installed capacity is about 70,000 TEU a year, but actual output is much lower: The Core puts it at 25,000–35,000 TEU.

The main problem is cost. According to The Core, Corten (weathering) steel makes up 60–65% of a container's cost and is 10–15% dearer in India. As a result, Indian containers cost $500–1,000 more than Chinese ones. An older figure from December 2022 put an Indian container at about ₹1.46 lakh against about ₹73,000 for a Chinese one. That figure is dated, so treat it as background only. The per-container subsidy exists to close this gap.

Where small suppliers fit in container manufacturing

Seedhi baat: you won't build a container plant with ₹20 lakh. But each plant buys corner castings, wooden floors, paint, door hardware, gaskets, fabricated sub-assemblies and logistics. The projected 50,000+ indirect jobs are in exactly these areas. And the 70–90% local-content rule means plants have to buy many of these parts in India.

Option 1: Component supply

Supply a part, or trade in a consumable, that container makers need: marine-grade paint and coatings, rubber door gaskets, locking-rod hardware, floor panels or fasteners. You don't need a foundry to begin. Many founders start by trading and, once orders are steady, move into small-scale making.

Right for: people with engineering-trade contacts in Rajkot, Faridabad or the NCR. See our guides to engineering goods in Rajkot and auto components in Faridabad. Both clusters already have the supplier ecosystem this work needs.

Option 2: Fabrication job-work

Take on welding, cutting and sub-assembly work (panels, doors, frames, cross-members) for a large plant or for makers of specialised containers. Paise kaise aayenge: you charge per piece or per job, and the client often supplies the steel, so your working capital stays lower. The light engineering goods cluster in North Delhi is a good model for how job-work units run.

Right for: people with welding or fabrication experience and a site within trucking distance of a container plant.

Option 3: Container refurbishing and conversion

This one doesn't depend on CMPS at all. Used containers need repair, repainting and certification, and converted containers sell as site offices, storage units, cafés and cabins. Yards near ports and ICDs (inland container depots) such as Mundra, Nhava Sheva, Chennai, Visakhapatnam, Dadri and Tughlakabad have steady supply. The repair skills are similar to those in automobile refurbishing in New Delhi: cutting, welding, painting and finishing.

Who should skip this

  • Anyone hoping to claim the CMPS subsidy directly with less than factory-scale capital. The 25,000 and 1 lakh TEU thresholds rule you out.
  • Founders with no fabrication or engineering background and no partner who has one.
  • Anyone who needs income within 6 months. Plants are still 2–3 years away, because the setup phase only starts in FY 2027-28.

Launch roadmap for a first-time founder

  1. Pick your lane (supply, job-work or refurbishing) based on your skills and where you live.
  2. Get Udyam registration so you count as an MSME and can use MSME schemes.
  3. Start with refurbishing or general fabrication so you have revenue while the CMPS plants are being built.
  4. Get quality systems in place. Large plants building to ISO 1496 and CSC standards will expect documented quality, so ISO 9001 helps.
  5. Look into finance: PMEGP (manufacturing projects up to ₹50 lakh, with a 15–35% margin-money subsidy depending on your category) and CGTMSE-backed collateral-free loans. The 3% CMPS interest subvention is for big plants only.
  6. Get onto vendor lists early. Contact DCM, Kalyani and Jupiter Wagons and ask about their vendor registration process.
  7. Watch shipmin.gov.in for the guidelines, the per-container rate and the testing-facility locations, and update your plan when they come out.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme the same as CMAS?

Yes. The Union Budget 2026-27 on 1 February 2026 announced a ₹10,000 crore container scheme, and early material called it the Container Manufacturing Assistance Scheme (CMAS). When the Cabinet approved it on 28 October 2026, it was renamed the Container Manufacturing Promotion Scheme (CMPS), and the timeline became FY 2027-28 to FY 2039-40.

How do I apply for the container manufacturing promotion scheme?

You can't apply yet. As of 31 October 2026 there is no portal, no application window and no published guidelines. An Inter-Ministerial Steering Committee chaired by the MoPSW Secretary still has to approve the guidelines. Watch shipmin.gov.in and PIB releases from the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways for updates, and don't pay anyone who claims to file applications now.

What is the minimum capacity to qualify for CMPS?

A new (greenfield) plant needs at least 1 lakh TEU of annual capacity, and an expansion (brownfield) must add at least 25,000 TEU a year. Several reports link these thresholds specifically to the 3% interest subvention. Domestic value addition must also rise step by step from 70% to 90%. The final guidelines will confirm eligibility for each part of the scheme.

How much subsidy will be given per container?

The per-container rate hasn't been published. The scheme sets aside ₹8,891 crore as operational expenditure assistance, paid for each container that is built to global shipping standards and sold. The exact rate should appear in the guidelines the steering committee approves, so treat any number quoted before then as unconfirmed.

Which are the main container manufacturing companies in India?

DCM Shriram (DCM Containers) made India's first EXIM container in July 2026 and has a 1,000-unit order from Maersk. Kalyani Cast-Tech makes ISO and specialised containers and corner castings. Jupiter Wagons makes ISO marine and energy-storage containers. Adani Ports was reported in May 2026 to be evaluating entry. CONCOR buys and operates containers but doesn't make them.

Can MSMEs benefit from the container manufacturing scheme?

Mostly indirectly. The capacity thresholds of 25,000 and 1 lakh TEU put direct claims out of reach for small units. But the 70–90% local-content rule pushes large plants to buy Indian components such as corner castings, wooden floors, paint, gaskets and fabricated sub-assemblies. MSMEs can benefit as suppliers, as job-work fabricators, or through container refurbishing.
