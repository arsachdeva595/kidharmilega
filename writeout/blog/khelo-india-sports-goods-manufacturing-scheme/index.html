Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme: Eligibility, Subsidy and What Small Units Can Do Now

The Cabinet cleared the ₹1,856.67 crore Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme on 28 October 2026. You can't apply yet. Here's who qualifies, why most of the money is meant for large investors, which sports products suit a ₹2.5–20 lakh budget, and how to use PMEGP while the guidelines are pending.

October 31, 2026 · 8 min read

The Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme is a ₹1,856.67 crore Central Sector Scheme. The Union Cabinet approved it on 28 October 2026, and it runs from FY 2026-27 to 2033-34. It will fund new sports goods manufacturing zones, upgrades to existing clusters such as Jalandhar and Meerut, international certification, R&D and exports. You cannot apply yet because there is no portal. The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports still has to notify the operational guidelines, and they should appear on yas.gov.in. The official announcement is the PIB Cabinet release.

Agar tumhare paas ₹2.5–20 lakh ki savings hai aur sports goods ka business soch rahe ho, yeh page tumhare liye hai. It covers what the scheme can realistically do for a micro unit, which products fit your budget, and how to start with PMEGP now instead of waiting for the guidelines.

TL;DR

  • Outlay: ₹1,856.67 crore, spread over FY 2026-27 to 2033-34
  • Run by: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (Department of Sports), with DPIIT, MSME, MSDE, ANRF and the Export Promotion Mission as partners
  • The biggest incentives are for big investors: news reports put the thresholds at ₹100 crore for anchor support and ₹10 crore for turnover-linked support
  • Where small units fit: cluster common facilities, start-up R&D grants of up to ₹20 lakh, skilling and expo/export support
  • What to do now: get Udyam registration, start on PMEGP, and keep checking yas.gov.in for the guidelines. Don't trust any "apply now" page until the ministry publishes one.

What the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme funds

Information & Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced the scheme at the post-Cabinet briefing on 28 October 2026. It is much bigger than the roughly ₹500 crore "dedicated initiative" that was reported after the Union Budget 2026-27 on 1 February 2026. Here is how the money is split, based on news reports that quote PIB. Check the PIB backgrounder for the final wording.

The SGMZs are meant to put manufacturers, suppliers, testing labs and design centres in one place, using the BHAVYA scheme and NICDC industrial parks. The government hasn't announced how many zones there will be or where. One reposted PIB backgrounder mentions about 4 zones of 300+ acres each, but it uses hedged language, so treat that as unconfirmed.

Eligibility: who qualifies under the scheme

The big-ticket incentives

According to The Bridge and Kashmir Images, there are two main incentives:

  • Anchor Investment Support: 20% capex support for anchor investors who invest ₹100 crore or more inside an SGMZ
  • Turnover-Linked Support: up to 15% for manufacturers who invest ₹10 crore or more

As of 31 October 2026, we hadn't been able to verify these percentages and thresholds against the official release text, so they may change when the guidelines come out. One source also says the government will pay 70% of the cost of a first international certification and 50% of renewals. That figure hasn't been confirmed anywhere else.

Seedha jawab for a ₹2.5–20 lakh founder

The headline incentives are not meant for you. A ₹10 crore investment is 50 times a ₹20 lakh budget. Policy Edge has made the same point: the zones aren't named yet, and the incentive design may favour large firms.

The scheme can still help a micro unit in three ways:

  1. Cluster common facilities. If your unit is in or near Jalandhar or Meerut, shared facilities such as testing and design centres cut costs you could never cover on your own.
  2. Start-up R&D grants of up to ₹20 lakh. These are open to DPIIT-registered sports start-ups. A new product idea, like better shin guards or a low-cost kabaddi mat, is the route here.
  3. Demand and visibility. The India Sports Expo, the Brand India campaign and export support help small suppliers find buyers. Existing RoDTEP export benefits also continue.

Why Meerut and Jalandhar matter

Small and micro firms make about 90% of India's sports goods, according to PIB. Jalandhar and Meerut together have 250+ exporting units, 1,000+ units selling in India, 4,000+ micro units and about 20,000 household units. Meerut is also Uttar Pradesh's ODOP district for sports goods.

  • India exports about $275 million of sports equipment a year. The global market is about $52 billion.
  • Indian brands hold about 75–80% of the global cricket bat market.
  • The official projection is $8.1 billion in exports and about 54 lakh jobs by 2036. The Tribune puts the expected investment at about ₹6,800 crore.
  • The scheme also wants new product lines, such as archery bows, kabaddi mats and shooting pellets. The LA 2028 Olympics, the Ahmedabad 2030 Commonwealth Games and the Brisbane 2032 Olympics are cited as demand drivers.

Paise kaise aayenge

  • Job work for exporters: large Jalandhar and Meerut units outsource stitching and finishing. The margins are thin, but the cash flow is steady from the first month.
  • Schools, academies and state sports departments: bulk orders for balls, nets and mats
  • Local sports retailers and marketplaces: sell under your own brand at a higher margin, but plan for 45–60 day payment cycles

Products to skip on a small budget

Skip archery bows, shooting pellets, certified helmets and FIFA Quality-tested match balls. Testing and certification costs alone can use up a ₹20 lakh budget. Wait until the ₹105 crore certification support is open and you know whether micro units qualify.

How to stack the Khelo India scheme with PMEGP

As of 31 October 2026, PMEGP is the realistic source of money for a new micro unit. The Khelo India scheme comes later, as a source of cluster support, R&D grants and buyers.

  • Project cost: up to ₹50 lakh for manufacturing units
  • Subsidy (margin money): 15% urban and 25% rural for general category. It is 25% urban and 35% rural for special categories: SC/ST/OBC, women, minorities, ex-servicemen, persons with disabilities, and founders in NER, hill and border areas or aspirational districts.
  • Own contribution: 10% for general category, 5% for special categories
  • Conditions: you must be 18 or older, Class VIII pass for manufacturing projects above ₹10 lakh, and it must be a new unit. Apply on the PMEGP e-portal on the KVIC website.

Worked example: a woman founder sets up a ₹15 lakh football and volleyball unit in a rural block of Meerut district.

  • She puts in 5%, which is ₹75,000.
  • The bank lends ₹14.25 lakh.
  • The PMEGP subsidy at 35% is ₹5.25 lakh. It is kept as a deposit with the bank for 3 years and then adjusted against the loan.

Once the guidelines are out, she can use the cluster's testing facility and look at the certification support for export orders.

Ek warning: government schemes usually don't allow two subsidies on the same machine or asset. Use PMEGP for your own capex. Expect Khelo India support to come through shared facilities, grants or performance incentives, not a second payment on the same machine. The final rules depend on the MoYAS guidelines.

Launch roadmap: what to do before the guidelines arrive

  1. Pick one product and one cluster from the table above. Spend 3–4 days in Meerut's or Jalandhar's sports market talking to traders about prices and payment terms.
  2. Get Udyam registration on udyamregistration.gov.in. It's free and you'll need it for every scheme.
  3. Write a project report covering machines, raw material, 10–15 karigars, working capital for 3 months and a 24-month breakeven plan.
  4. Apply under PMEGP and finish the mandatory EDP training.
  5. Join the local manufacturers' association. Cluster upgrade money will most likely go through cluster bodies and state governments.
  6. If you're building a new product, register as a DPIIT start-up so you're ready for the ₹20 lakh R&D grant.
  7. Check yas.gov.in and PIB every month for the operational guidelines and the list of SGMZ locations.
  8. Start with job work, then build your brand for retail or export by year 2.

Who this is right for, and who should skip it

Right for you if: your family has roots in Meerut, Jalandhar or a nearby district, you can manage a stitching or wood workshop, and you can wait 18–24 months to break even.

Skip it if: you're expecting a direct subsidy cheque from this scheme in 2026, you have no contacts with buyers, or you want a quick-return trading business.

How this differs from the Khelo India athlete scheme

The older Khelo India scheme funds athletes, sports infrastructure and competitions. This scheme is industrial policy. It funds factories, clusters, certification and exports, under the slogan "India Plays, India Manufactures!"

Frequently asked questions

Is there an online portal to apply for the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme?

No, not yet. The Cabinet approved the scheme on 28 October 2026, but the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports still has to notify the operational guidelines. Watch yas.gov.in and PIB for the official notification. Don't trust any website that claims to take applications or fees for this scheme before the ministry publishes the guidelines.

How much money does the Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme have?

The total outlay is ₹1,856.67 crore, often rounded to ₹1,857 crore, from FY 2026-27 to 2033-34. About ₹1,130 crore goes to manufacturing zones and the anchor and turnover-linked incentives. ₹105 crore goes to international certification support. The rest covers cluster upgrades, R&D, skilling and promotion.

Can small MSMEs benefit from the sports goods manufacturing scheme?

Partly. News reports put the main incentives at ₹100 crore for anchor investors and ₹10 crore for turnover-linked support, which is out of reach for micro units. Small units can benefit from shared facilities in clusters like Jalandhar and Meerut, start-up R&D grants of up to ₹20 lakh, skilling programmes, the India Sports Expo and export support.

Can I use PMEGP and the Khelo India sports goods scheme together?

Most likely yes, but in different ways. PMEGP gives a 15–35% margin money subsidy on a new unit's project cost of up to ₹50 lakh, and you can apply now. Khelo India support is expected through cluster facilities, grants and certification help. Claiming two subsidies on the same machine is usually not allowed, and the final rules depend on the MoYAS guidelines.

Where will the Sports Goods Manufacturing Zones be set up?

The government hasn't announced the number of zones or their locations. They will be built using the BHAVYA scheme and NICDC industrial parks. One reposted backgrounder mentions about 4 zones of 300+ acres each, but this is unconfirmed. Jalandhar and Meerut are expected to benefit through cluster upgrades, and officials have said new hubs will also be created.

How is this different from the regular Khelo India scheme?

The regular Khelo India scheme funds athletes, sports infrastructure and competitions. The Khelo India Sports Goods Manufacturing Scheme is industrial policy. It supports factories, clusters, certification, R&D and exports of sports equipment. It is run by the Department of Sports with DPIIT, the MSME ministry, MSDE and ANRF, and aims for $8.1 billion in sports goods exports by 2036.
